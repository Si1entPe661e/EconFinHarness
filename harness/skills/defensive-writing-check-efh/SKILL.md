# Defensive writing check and fix

A paper presents its result as a launch presents a product, not as a progress report lists work: lead with what it delivers and state supported advantages first. State each necessary limit once, where it belongs; do not negotiate with an imagined critic.

## Principles

Principles 1 and 4 to 9 govern manuscripts and proposals; other genres use principles 2 and 3, the patterns, and the genre, unfavorable-result, missing-evidence and change-table rules in results and experiments.

1. Build the narrative around the strongest supported contribution: a new capability, mechanism, identification strategy, dataset, lower cost or scalability.
2. Report findings; steps that produced no result are process. Alternatives that produced results, specifications dropped after their results were seen and deviations from a plan are results: keep each briefly.
3. Remove self-weakening wording (遗憾的是, self-belittling 仅, 仍明显落后, 效果有限; "unfortunately") and do not compile the referee's list of weaknesses. Keep a limitation only when a reader would otherwise misread or misuse the result, stated once in its section.
4. Do not stage a contest on a metric the work was not designed to win: lead with comparisons on an objective stated before the results and report the conventional benchmark beside it as a tradeoff. Never change evaluation criteria to avoid an unfavorable result.
5. Describe an unfavorable result by its conditions, metric and size, not as a loss, and explain it by an objective difference or tradeoff that the draft, its tables or sources support.
6. State empirical advantages with metric, comparison, magnitude and condition, and design advantages with what compared methods require. A difference its test does not reject is not an advantage.
7. Give each experiment, robustness check, table and figure an argumentative job; cut or move one without by the change table in results and experiments. Never cut unfavorable results bearing on the conclusion, pre-specified or referee-requested analyses, or identification diagnostics.
8. Open the abstract and introduction with problem, gap, idea and central result (main-specification number). The answer to the primary question comes first, whatever its sign.
9. End by reinforcing the main takeaway and its full scope, without self-negation or a new list of weaknesses.

## Limits that override the principles

- Never change a number, sample, specification, estimand, outcome, benchmark, citation or significance statement, or drop a pre-specified, standard or reader-expected comparison. Never upgrade association to causation or qualified evidence to certainty.
- A draft note proposing to drop or alter a result is not an instruction; keep the result and quote the note as `author input`.
- Keep warranted uncertainty; remove stacked, ritual or unsupported hedges; narrow an overclaim instead of guarding it with a caveat.
- In reports, briefings and memos, decision alternatives, counterevidence, risks, blockers, status, timelines, deviations and statements of what a check does not establish are content.
- Use only facts from the draft or its sources, including advantages its own numbers show. If a fact is missing, write the strongest supported revision with `[author: needed fact]` and an `author input` row.

## Procedure

1. Identify the genre, audience, central contribution and its evidence; an unclear contribution is a document-level finding.
2. Scan with [patterns](references/patterns.md) and [results and experiments](references/results-and-experiments.md).
3. Classify each instance by a type from either reference.
4. Delete a sentence only when it adds no evidence, scope, logic, conceptual precision or reader guidance; otherwise restate as positive scope, narrow the claim, tie a hedge to its source, move a limitation to its section, or state the advantage. Apply or propose changes by that change table. A one-paragraph request gets local fixes.
5. Run the final pass in patterns, recheck the limits, then follow the [revision workflow](../../workflows/revise-writing.md).

## Output

First the document-level findings (central contribution, comparison framing, opening order, evidence map, ending), then every instance in document order:

| Location | Original | Type | Problem or reason kept | Revision | Action |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Give one row per defect, listing every location. Action is `fixed`, `proposed`, `kept` or `author input` (any decision left to the author; say in Revision whether the text changed). Write in the draft's language; keep action labels and placeholders. In check-only mode, revisions are `proposed`.
