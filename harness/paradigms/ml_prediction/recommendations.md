# Recommended rules: `ml_prediction` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (2 validated recipe cards, one R and one Python, covering 3 designs and 8 specifications) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (6 paper-practice notes taken from the paper text, 5 of them with a linked recipe card). This evidence base is thin. Two cards and six notes cannot establish a convention, one of the two cards has only minimal coverage, and several facets in `observed.md` are unknown for one of the two papers, so every corpus pointer below is an illustration of where a rule has been applied or not applied, never a frequency argument. Observed and reported frequencies are evidence of practice only, not of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a tag absent from a note or a null card field means "not recorded", not "not done". Each rule states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid + record id + path:lines from `observed.md`; artid + page + locator from `reported.md`; `_all:` prefix for pointers taken from `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`) and the validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; check records live in `harness_build/pilot/runs/`.

## Applicability

- Covered: supervised prediction models (regression or classification, including text-derived and LLM-derived measures) fit on observed data and used either to construct a variable that enters a downstream estimand, or as the reported object of study. Covers cross-section, panel and repeated cross-section data, and covers prediction steps embedded inside a causal design (proxies for conditional average treatment effects, imputed characteristics, policy scores) as far as the prediction step itself is concerned.
- Not covered: the causal design that surrounds the prediction step. Identification of the downstream estimand belongs to the method of that design (`did`, `iv`, `rdd`, `rct`) and to its own recommendations file. Also not covered: unsupervised methods with no target label (clustering, topic models used purely descriptively), structural estimation with a simulated model, forecast-combination and predictive-regression evaluation as an econometric object (see the separate `predictive_regression` reported layer), and the choice of learner class on grounds of predictive performance alone.

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 6 notes in `reported.md`; a note that lacks a tag did not record it, and with six notes no count here supports a claim about the field. Pointers are artid, page range and locator exactly as printed in `reported.md`; the statements are the reader's paraphrases, so re-read the page before citing.

- r1 (state the role, the estimand and the imputation assumption): 3/6 notes record `identifying_assumption_explicit`, 1/6 `estimand_named`, 1/6 `design_variant_named`, 2/6 `threats_discussed`. Pointers: qje_140_1_3 p.13-14 Section III.B; ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.3-8 Introduction; Section 2.2; rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.3-9 Introduction; Section 1.2 Motivation for modeling choices. Divergence: the papers that state an assumption state it for the imputation step, and only one note records the downstream estimand being named as such, so the practice covers the second half of the rule more often than the first. In the other direction, one note records a paper framing its approach as deliberately agnostic about whether the fitted proxy is consistent for the underlying object (ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.8-10 Section 2.3, tagged `design_variant_named`), which is a stronger position than r1 requires and which r1 does not describe.
- r2 (partition protocol, hyperparameters, seed, cross-fitting): no note records a train/validation/test protocol, a fold count, a hyperparameter value or a seed; the only related tags are `software_named` 3/6 (ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.38-38 Section 6.3, Comment 6.4; ecta_2026_ecta21180 p.13-14 Section 4.2) and `other:covariate_selection` 1/6 (ecta_2026_ecta21180 p.13-14 Section 4.2, candidate covariates narrowed by two importance criteria). The code layer is where the protocol is visible (ecta_2025_ecta19303 code1.R:59-61; qje_140_1_3 tune_utils.py:115-116 and :137-138), so on this rule the papers are quieter than the code. `_all:` shows one note in the wider corpus recording a fully specified ensemble in the text (restud_2026_restud_rdag029 p.20-20 Section 5.4 and footnote 9), which is what r2 asks for. Divergence: practice as recorded does not meet this rule in the main text, and a reader who only has the paper cannot refit the model.
- r3 (time-ordered split, no look-ahead): 2/6 notes record `sample_period_stated`, and only rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.11-15 Section 2; Table 3 notes states a data period and a separate out-of-sample test period with the gap explained; qje_140_1_3 p.17-17 Section IV.A states a single study year chosen because outcomes were most fully resolved. No note records a look-ahead or leakage check by name. Divergence: the rule's central check is absent from the reported layer, and the two cards use random rather than time-ordered splits, so this rule rests on the method sources and not on corpus practice.
- r4 (external validation of the predicted measure before use): 2/6 notes have any `diagnostic_reported` entry at all; within them, 1/6 records `other:calibration_check` (qje_140_1_3 p.19-20 Figure I, predicted probabilities against self-reported labels in a matched validation sample), 1/6 `other:match_representativeness_check` (qje_140_1_3 p.30-31 Section VI, footnote), 1/6 `other:goodness_of_fit_diagnostic` (ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.32-33 Table II, candidate learners compared before one is chosen) and 1/6 `site_specific` under external validity (qje_140_1_3 p.18-31 Section IV.B; Section VI robustness). Divergence in the paper's favour: one paper performs the validation, the representativeness check and the external-validity caveat together, which is more than r4's minimum; and one paper reports a learner-comparison table before settling on a main specification (`baseline_then_variants` 1/6, ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.32-33 Table II), a step r4 does not require. Divergence against: no note records accuracy broken out by the groups the downstream analysis compares, and no note records agreement with human coders.
- r5 (propagate first-stage uncertainty): 2/6 notes have any `inference_justification` entry; 1/6 records `other:quantile_aggregated_inference` and 1/6 `other:coverage_calibration_by_simulation` (both ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.18-24 Section 4 and p.23-24 Section 4.4), and 2/6 record `multiple_testing_adjustment` (ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.16-17 Section 3.4; restud_2026_restud_rdag080 p.23-23 Section 6.2, where a forest is motivated partly as a way to avoid testing many candidate dimensions). Reader gaps record that no clustering level or few-cluster discussion is stated in these papers, and the concordance shows `se_type` paper_only 3 and code_only 1 (qje_140_1_3 s3, where the code requests robust analytic errors that the text does not describe). Divergence: split-aggregated inference in one paper goes further than r5's minimum, while the code-only robust-error case is exactly the situation r5 asks to be labelled and which the text does not label.
- r6 (report the prediction step; state the measurement caveat; tabulate prediction-step robustness): 3/6 notes record `software_named`, 3/6 `code_public` and 3/6 `replication_package_cited`; 2/6 record `identification_caveat`, 2/6 `interpretation_caveat`, 1/6 `external_validity` and 1/6 `measurement_error_discussed`. Pointers: ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.35-35 Section 6.2 (associations from a classification analysis flagged as possibly reflecting something other than the named construct); qje_140_1_3 p.47-47 Conclusion; aer_114_2_5 p.11-20 Section I footnote 18 and Section III footnote 28 (a predicted-benefits variable whose measurement error is discussed in footnotes). On robustness placement, 2/6 record `in_appendix` and 2/6 `online_appendix_not_available`, while rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.33-46 Section 5.6; Table 12; Appendix C Table C1 keeps hyperparameter robustness in a main-text table, which is what r6 asks for. Divergence: the caveat half of the rule is met more often than the reporting half, the measurement-error discussion sits in footnotes rather than in a limitations paragraph, and prediction-step robustness is more often summarised in prose with the tables in a supplement that the reader could not obtain.

## Open questions for review

- Evidence base. Two cards and six notes, with one card at minimal coverage and one paper (aer_114_2_5) having no card at all. The reviewer should decide whether a rule set is publishable at this size, or whether this file should be held until more `ml_prediction` cards and notes exist. No rule here should be cited as corpus-supported convention.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 5 notes with cards, 63 items): agree 30, paper_only 28, code_only 2, disagree 1, unknown 2. paper_only is concentrated in comparison_group (4), controls (3), data_source (3), robustness (3) and se_type (3). Implication for trusting card fields: `estimator` (agree 9, paper_only 2, disagree 0) and `fixed_effects` (agree 5) are the fields that can be cited from a card; `controls`, `robustness`, `data_source`, `weights` and `se_type` are recording gaps at least as often as they are substantive absences, so `observed.md` counts for them must not be read as evidence that a step was skipped. The single disagreement is an output count (restud_2026_restud_rdag080), which affects only output mapping, not the rules here.
- Scope boundary with `predictive_regression` and with the surrounding causal method. Several of these papers are causal designs with a prediction step inside them (heterogeneity proxies, policy trees, imputed characteristics). The reviewer must decide whether r1's role distinction is the right cut, or whether "prediction inside a causal design" should be its own paradigm with its own file.
- LLM-derived and text-derived measures are named in the method notes for this paradigm but appear in none of the six notes; r4's human-coder benchmark and r2's seed and version requirements for LLM labelling (temperature, prompt version, model snapshot) are written from the method sources and from general practice, not from this corpus. The reviewer should decide whether to keep them here or to split an `llm_measurement` branch.
- r5 leaves open which correction to use when the prediction is fit on the same sample as the second stage and the pipeline cannot be resampled at acceptable cost. Options: cross-fitting with split-aggregated intervals, a prediction-powered correction requiring a labelled subsample, or reporting the conditional interval with an explicit caveat. This is a user or reviewer decision, not a default.
- Numeric thresholds are deliberately absent (no minimum fold count, no minimum labelled-sample size, no accuracy floor). If the harness wants defaults, they must come from a source or be declared harness conventions.
- Validation. No template exists for this paradigm, so r2 to r5 are marked `no template yet` rather than tested; r1 and r6 are reporting rules that no template can test. A reviewer should say whether a template covering split protocol, seed capture and pipeline-level resampling is worth building before this file leaves draft.
- Hold-out. `ml_prediction` has no hold-out list, so no exclusion applies to either layer; the reviewer should confirm this is intended before any evaluation is built on these papers.
