# Recommended rules: `rdd` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed in `harness/skills/rdd/references.md` and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (distillation set of 10 recipe cards; held-out cards listed in harness/paradigms/rdd/reported.md are not used there) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (11 paper-practice notes taken from the paper text, not the code; the same paper-level hold-out as for the cards applies, so the four notes on held-out papers are excluded from this method's reported layer and count only in `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`). Observed frequencies and reported frequencies are both evidence of practice only, not of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a tag absent from a note or a null card field means "not recorded", not "not done". Every rule states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid + record id from `observed.md`; artid + page + locator from `reported.md`) and the validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; historical build and review records are not distributed.

## Applicability

- Covered: sharp and fuzzy RD with a continuous or moderately discrete running variable, one cutoff (or several cutoffs normalised to one), cross-section or panel data with clusters; regression kink designs for the estimator and bandwidth rules only.
- Not covered: difference-in-discontinuities, bunching estimands, geographic boundary designs (two-dimensional running variables), dynamic RD with repeated crossings.

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 11 notes in `reported.md`; a note that lacks a tag did not record it. Pointers are artid, page range and locator as printed in `reported.md`; re-read the page before citing. This section records only which procedures papers run, where they report them and in what form. It records no outcome of any check, no estimate, and no statement about what a paper concluded; where the underlying note in `reported.md` phrases a practice through its result, the practice is restated here as procedure and reporting location. The four notes on held-out papers (listed under "Open questions for review") are excluded from this method's reported layer and are not used in any count or pointer below.

- r1 (local linear, MSE bandwidth, robust bias-corrected CI): the estimator is the item on which paper and card agree most often (estimator agree 13 items, paper_only 1, disagree 0). 1/11 notes record `other:robust_bias_corrected_rd` (rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.30-33 Section 4.2, Table 6 notes) and 1/11 `other:rdrobust_robust_se_default` (restud_2025_restud_rdae108 p.10-11 Section 3.4.1, footnote 11; Appendix Table D.6). Papers go beyond the rule by placing the local-linear and higher-order columns side by side in the main tables (aer_110_2_5 p.17-17 Tables 2-8, `alt_estimator` 4/11); the earlier observation of two named bandwidth criteria reported next to each other came from a held-out note and is no longer cited here. Honest CIs appear as a paper-stated alternative in 1/11 (see r6), which r1 does not mention.
- r2 (report bandwidth, kernel, effective N, SE and cluster choice): 6/11 notes record `se_type_in_notes`, 5/11 `cluster_level_in_notes`, 7/11 `n_reported`, 3/11 `weights_stated` (the triangular kernel stated as a weighting choice), 1/11 `other:bandwidth_reported_per_column` and 1/11 `other:kernel_and_polynomial_stated`. Pointers: restud_2025_restud_rdae108 p.12-12 Table 1 and notes; aer_110_2_5 p.16-24 Notes to Tables 2-8; aer_114_11_1 p.11-11 Section III. This contradicts the picture from the cards, which leave SE type unknown in 3/10 and cluster dimensions unknown in 7/10: the papers state these in table notes (cluster_level paper_only 3, se_type paper_only 1 in the concordance), so the card gaps are recording gaps, not reporting gaps. Papers also state the bandwidth and kernel in the notes or the text rather than in a machine-readable file; only one note records treated and control counts per table (rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.22-46 Tables 3 to 12), and none records the effective N on each side of the bandwidth as such.
- r3 (density test and covariate balance before the main estimate): 9/11 notes record `density_test` and 9/11 `balance_table`, with the checks placed `in_main_text` in 9/11 and `in_appendix` in 10/11 (usually a summary in the text and the full table in the appendix). Pointers: aer_114_11_1 p.14-14 Section III; aer_110_2_5 p.13-13 Table 1; rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.23-30 Section 4.1, Table 4. This goes well beyond what the code cards show (density test call present in 2/10, not found in 2, unknown in 4): in the concordance the diagnostic item is paper_only in 8 cases against agree in 8, and one reader gap notes a card marked not_found_in_scope for a density test the paper reports. Papers also add checks the rule lacks: a joint permutation test of covariate continuity (restud_2025_restud_rdae108 p.16-16 Section 4.1; Appendix Table C.8), an attrition or match-rate discontinuity check (aer_114_11_1 p.11-11 Section II, Combining Data Sources) and a paragraph that walks through individual rows of the balance table rather than reporting the joint test alone (rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.28-29 Section 4.1 end, Figure IA-1).
- r4 (bandwidth grid, polynomial-order variant, placebo cutoffs): 7/11 notes record `bandwidth_sensitivity`, 5/11 `alt_bandwidth`, 7/11 `alt_specification`, 6/11 `placebo_cutoffs` and 7/11 `placebo_outcomes`. Pointers: aer_110_2_5 p.26-27 Section III.F; Figure 5; Appendix Table B3; aer_114_11_1 p.28-29 Figure 4, Section IV.E Placebo Checks. Cards link a robustness entry in only 2/10 (robustness paper_only 8 in the concordance), so the paper layer is the only corpus evidence for this rule's placebo part. Papers go beyond the rule with placebo outcomes (7/11), leave-one-out sample checks (qje_2026_qje_qjaf045 p.26-26 Section III.C.3) and by presenting the grid as a coefficient plot (`coefficient_plot` 9/11) rather than a table.
- r5 (cluster at the assignment or sampling level, report clusters, plan for few): 1/11 notes record `cluster_at_assignment_level` (aer_114_11_1 p.11-11 Section III, footnote 11), 3/11 `cluster_at_unit_level` (qje_2026_qje_qjaf045 p.19-19 Section III.B.1), and 4/11 `no_justification_found` (jpe_2026_740226 p.17-17 Table 1 note; qje_2026_qje_qjaf045 p.22-50 Sections III.C and IV). 2/11 record `randomization_inference` as a robustness alternative (restud_2025_restud_rdae108 p.2-24 Introduction; Section 4.3.3; Appendix F), 1/11 `spatial_hac` (aer_110_2_5 p.14-14 Section II.B, footnote 23) and 1/11 a re-clustering robustness row (qje_2026_qje_qjaf045 p.25-25 Table III, Panel A). No note records the number of clusters inside the bandwidth; one reader gap states that a few-cluster discussion is absent and two more record no stated clustering level or rationale for the baseline standard errors; the rule's cluster-count requirement therefore has no reported basis and the randomization-inference alternative is a paper practice the rule does not mention.
- r6 (honest CIs with a discrete running variable): 1/11 notes record `other:honest_ci` (restud_2026_restud_rdag071 p.25-27 Section 4.5; Table 3 column (5)); no note among the 11 records a smoothness-bound choice or sensitivity bounds (the earlier observations of both came from a held-out note and appear only in `_all`); one note records a sample-exclusion rule defined on a region of the running variable, with the reason stated in a footnote (restud_2026_restud_rdag071 p.18-18 footnote 28). No distillation card implements honest CIs, so the paper layer is the only corpus evidence for this rule. The one note that records an honest CI reports it as an additional column beside a conventional estimate; r6 was rewritten in this revision to match that reporting form and to make the honest-interval clause conditional on a discreteness diagnostic, instead of the earlier absolute "do not rely on" wording.
- r7 (fuzzy designs: first stage, complier interpretation): 5/11 notes record `first_stage_table` (aer_114_11_1 p.12-13 Table 1, Figure 1; restud_2025_restud_rdae108 p.12-22 Figure 2; Table 3 panel c), no note among the 11 records `weak_iv_statistic` (two reader gaps note its absence), 1/11 `compliers_described` (aer_110_2_5 p.18-18 Section III, discussion following Table 3), 2/11 `instrument_exclusion_argued` (restud_2026_restud_rdag071 p.23-24 Section 4.3.4, equation (3)) and 5/11 `compliers_vs_population`. Cards show a first-stage call in 2/10. Papers go beyond the rule by stacking first stage, reduced form and IV panels in one table with a control mean and an elasticity row (restud_2026_restud_rdag071 p.26-26 Table 2) and by comparing complier characteristics with the population (see r11).
- r8 (RD plot from code, table columns mapped to specification ids): 8/11 notes record `rd_plot`, 4/11 `binscatter`, 4/11 `raw_data_plot` and 9/11 `coefficient_plot`. Pointers: aer_114_11_1 p.12-25 Figures 1-3; aer_110_2_5 p.11-11 Figure 2 notes (bin widths and the fitted polynomial stated in the figure notes); restud_2025_restud_rdaf004 p.16-29 Figures 2 to 9; Tables 2, 3 (the `rd_plot` tag covering the figures together with the tables they accompany); restud_2025_restud_rdae108 p.3-10 Figure 1; Appendix Figure B.2 (raw-data plot). The paper layer supports the plot half of the rule; the mapping half cannot be observed in paper text, and the concordance for outputs (paper_only 7, agree 2, disagree 1) shows that cards record far fewer outputs than the papers present. r8 is therefore split in this revision into a sourced plot clause and a harness-convention mapping clause that is marked unvalidated.

## Open questions for review

- An earlier research review raises twelve further objections to r1 to r8 (CER versus MSE bandwidths for inference, reporting the bias-corrected estimate, mass-point handling options, density-test exceptions, bandwidth fixing under bootstrap, who sets the smoothness bound for honest CIs, fuzzy monotonicity and the sharpbw option, rdplot's global polynomial default); each needs a decision before this document can leave draft.
- Numeric thresholds in the discrete branch (100 support points in the sample; 20 and 10 inside the bandwidth per side) are harness conventions, not literature values; the full text of Kolesar and Rothe (2018) was not read.
- Whether to recommend covariate adjustment by default or only as a robustness variant; the corpus cards do not record covariate use in RD calls consistently.
- Choice between common and side-specific MSE bandwidths; the observed cards do not ground the selector option in most calls.
- Small-cluster inference inside narrow bandwidths: no corpus evidence yet; rule r5 relies on the general clustering literature.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 11 notes, 136 items): agree 67, paper_only 55, unknown 10, disagree 4, code_only 0. paper_only is concentrated in data_source (9), diagnostic (8), robustness (8), outputs (7), comparison_group (4) and controls (4). Implication: a null card field or an `unknown` diagnostic status is a recording gap and must not be read as absence; observed.md counts for diagnostics, robustness, controls and fixed effects understate practice and should not be cited as evidence that a check was skipped. The estimator field (agree 13, disagree 0) and se_type (agree 6, paper_only 1) are the card fields that can be trusted most.
- The 4 disagreements concern controls and fixed effects in restud_2025_restud_rdaf004 s10 (card records covariates and an absorbed fixed effect where the paper states a bare baseline), one output count (restud_2026_restud_rdag071) and one robustness entry (jpe_2026_740226). Implication: a card specification may be a variant rather than the baseline, and rules that lean on card `controls` or `fixed_effects` (r9) need the paper text or the card role field before citing a card as a baseline.
- Four notes (aer_107_1_5, ecta_92_3_6, jpe_132_9_2, qje_140_1_11) come from papers held out for the rdd skill evaluation. Under the paper-level hold-out they are excluded from this method's reported layer (the "Excluded" line under "## Inputs" in `reported.md`) and appear only in `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`; no count or pointer in this document uses them. The reviewer should confirm that this keeps the hold-out clean for the rdd skill evaluation.
- Rules r9 to r12 are reporting and interpretation rules taken from paper practice. r9's bandwidth, kernel, effective N and SE fields ride on the r2 test; everything else in r9 to r12 carries no method source for the reporting convention itself and is marked in the validation column as not testable by a template, except the control-side mean in r12, which is computable but is not computed by the current template. They need a reviewer decision on whether reporting rules belong in this document or in the writer role's conventions.
- Significance stars split the notes (stars_used 5/11 against no_stars 4/11), so no rule is written; the writer role's convention decides.
- Placement tags (`in_main_text`, `in_appendix`, `in_footnote`) are reader judgements about the paper text and cannot be validated against code; r10 rests on them alone.
- Raised by the independent review of 2026-09-08 and still open for a person: (i) the numeric threshold that makes a cluster count "few" inside an RD bandwidth, and whether the small-cluster interval is primary or a robustness row (r5 clause b); (ii) whether an honest confidence interval is the primary inference or a reported sensitivity when the discreteness diagnostic fires, and who sets the smoothness bound (r6); (iii) whether a column-to-specification manifest should become a machine-checkable contract, which would move r8 clause (b) out of "not testable by a template" (owner: code-engineer). Historical review correspondence is not distributed.
