# Recommended rules: `matching` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from one generated layer of corpus practice: reported paper practice in `reported.md` (11 paper-practice notes, 10 of them with a linked recipe card). There is no observed layer for this method: no recipe card carries `matching` as its design method, so `observed.md` and `observed.json` do not exist and no card field is counted here; the only code-side evidence available is the "Paper versus code" concordance printed inside `reported.md`, which is cited as such. The evidence base is thin and it is also skewed: in many of the 11 notes the matching step is a way of building a comparison group that then feeds a difference-in-differences or event study, or an alternative estimator offered as robustness, rather than the paper's headline design (`robustness_reported` `alt_estimator` 5/11, `alt_control_group` 4/11). Every count below is out of 11 unless prefixed `_all:`, which refers to `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md` (91 notes, all methods). Reported frequencies are evidence of practice, never of correctness: there is no majority threshold, and a tag absent from a note means the reader did not record it, not that the paper omitted it. The rules therefore rest on the method literature and on judgement; the corpus supplies pointers and shows where practice and rule diverge. Each rule states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid + page + locator from `reported.md`) and the validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; check records live in `harness_build/pilot/runs/`.

## Applicability

- Covered: selection-on-observables designs in which a comparison group is constructed by matching or reweighting on pre-treatment covariates, including one-to-one and one-to-many nearest-neighbour matching on a propensity score or on a distance metric, exact and coarsened exact matching, matching with or without replacement and with or without a caliper, entropy balancing and other covariate-balancing weights when they are used as a substitute for matching, and the common case in this corpus where the matched sample is the input to a difference-in-differences, stacked-event-study or event-study specification.
- Covered for the matching step only: designs where matching selects the comparison units and the estimator that follows belongs to another paradigm (`did`, `event_study`, `return_event_study`, `synthetic_control`). The rules below govern the construction of the matched sample, its balance evidence, the weights it implies and the inference that follows; the outcome model's own rules stay with that paradigm.
- Not covered / different branch: propensity-score weighting used purely as a nuisance-parameter step inside a doubly robust or machine-learning estimator (`ml_nuisance`), synthetic control and synthetic difference-in-differences donor construction (`synthetic_control`), record linkage between data sources where the goal is to identify the same unit rather than to build a comparison group, and matched-pair designs created by randomisation inside an experiment (`rct`).

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 11 notes in `reported.md`; a note that lacks a tag did not record it. Pointers are artid, page range and locator exactly as printed in `reported.md`; the statements there are the reader's paraphrases, so re-read the page before citing. Pointers prefixed `_all:` come from `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md` (91 notes).

- r1 (estimand, conditional independence, overlap, and the limits of matching). 3/11 notes record `estimand_named`, 2/11 `identifying_assumption_explicit`, 4/11 `threats_discussed` and 4/11 `design_variant_named`. Pointers: aer_111_12_2 p.1-4 Abstract and Introduction (design named and the estimand stated in the introduction); rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf080 p.3-4 Introduction, paragraphs on benchmark borrowers (identifying assumption and the main threat named up front); qje_140_1_4 p.36-37 Section III.B.2 (the identification threat named as the motivation for the matched design). Practice diverges from the rule in two directions. It falls short of it in that no note records a statement that the matched estimate is local to treated units that find a match, and the population the estimate describes is usually left implicit; it goes beyond the rule in that one paper states why a sharper design was rejected before defending the matched one (aer_111_12_2 p.18-18 Section IIIA, footnote), which the rule does not ask for.
- r2 (full specification of the procedure). 4/11 notes record `matching_or_linkage_described`, 4/11 `exclusions_justified`, 2/11 `unit_of_observation_stated` and 2/11 `sample_period_stated`. Pointers: qje_140_1_4 p.36-37 Section III.B.2; jfe_2025_j_jfineco_2025_104150 p.11-12 Section 5.1; Table 5 and Table 6 notes; rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf080 p.12-13 Section 2.1.7, footnote 22 (the sample built as an ordered list of restrictions). The papers that describe the procedure describe it well, and one states the timing of the matching variables explicitly (restud_2026_restud_rdaf027 p.12-13 Section 4.1). No note in this method records a random seed or a tie-breaking rule, so the part of the rule that concerns reproducibility of the match itself has no reported basis here; the concordance shows `comparison_group` paper_only 6 against agree 1, meaning the card layer usually did not capture the comparison group at all and cannot be used to fill that gap.
- r3 (overlap and trimming). 1/11 notes record `other:common_support_trimming` (aer_111_12_2 p.18-19 Section IIIA) and 3/11 `sample_size_reported_per_table`, one of which reports the match rate by origin group and states that unmatched units are dropped (qje_140_1_4 p.36-37 Section III.B.2). No note records a plot or table of the score distribution for treated and comparison units before matching. This is a clear divergence: the rule asks for an overlap display that the reported layer does not show anywhere, and only one of the 11 papers is recorded as imposing and describing a support restriction.
- r4 (balance before and after, standardised differences, no reliance on balance tests). 7/11 notes record `balance_table`, with placement `in_main_text` 5/11 and `in_appendix` 5/11 (usually a summary in the text and the full table in the appendix). Pointers: aer_111_12_2 p.22-23 Table 1, columns 1 and 2; jfe_2025_j_jfineco_2025_104150 p.11-12 Table 5; qje_140_1_4 p.36-37 Section III.B.2. Practice diverges from the rule on the statistic used: the balance evidence recorded in these notes is built on mean differences with t-statistics or p-values rather than on standardised differences with a stated threshold, and one paper asserts balance qualitatively in the main text and relegates the formal table to an unavailable online appendix (qje_140_1_4 p.36-37 Section III.B.2). A cross-method note shows the interpretive hazard the rule guards against, arguing that one imbalanced covariate out of eleven is what chance would produce (`_all:` rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf079 p.20-20 Section 4.1, Table 2 discussion). Practice goes beyond the rule in one respect worth adopting: reporting balance on covariates that matching did not target (aer_111_12_2 p.22-23 Table 1, columns 1 and 2).
- r5 (matching as a step before DiD or an event study). 4/11 notes record `design_variant_named` and 1/11 `parallel_trends_argued` (rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf080 p.20-24 Section 3.1 end and Section 3.3, where each benchmark group is introduced with its strength and its limitation). Pointers: aer_111_12_2 p.1-4 Abstract and Introduction; jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.2-7 Section 1 (introduction) and Section 4.2; restud_2025_restud_rdaf004 p.12-13 Section 5.2. On weights the divergence runs against the rule: 2/11 notes record `weights_stated`, and in one of them the reported specification is deliberately unweighted with the matching weights moved to an appendix variant (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.10-10 Section 5.2, discussion around Table 5), while another records that no weights are mentioned in text or table notes even though matching is five nearest neighbours with replacement (rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf080 p.21-26 Section 3.2.1, Table 4 notes) and the concordance records `weights` code_only 1 for the same paper (rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf080 s1.weights), that is, a weight in the code that the text does not mention. No note in this method records mean reversion or regression to the mean as a named threat from matching on pre-treatment outcomes, so that half of the rule rests on the literature alone.
- r6 (inference after matching). The reported basis is essentially empty: `inference_justification` is recorded in 1/11 notes and only as `no_justification_found` (aer_113_6_2 p.1-37 throughout; the tag concerns multiple testing rather than matching). Several reader gaps in `reported.md` state that no justification for the clustering level appears in the main text. Cross-method pointers show what the papers in this set do state elsewhere: heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors for a cross-sectional regression (`_all:` aer_111_12_2 p.33-33 Table 4 notes), an alternative standard error reported as a robustness exercise rather than as the inference discussion (`_all:` aer_111_12_2 p.19-19 Section IIIA, footnote referencing appendix table), a cluster level chosen from the linked unit (`_all:` qje_140_1_4 p.44-44 Section III.B.4, footnote) and a bootstrap specific to a synthetic-control step (`_all:` jfe_2025_j_jfineco_2025_104150 p.15-15 Section 5.1, Fig. 6 notes). Practice diverges from the rule sharply: no note records the Abadie-Imbens variance, and none records treating reuse of comparison units under matching with replacement as a source of dependence. In the concordance, `cluster_level` agrees between paper and card in 8 items, so the cluster variable itself is usually traceable even when its justification is not stated.
- r7 (robustness across matching choices, plus sensitivity to unobservables). 5/11 notes record `alt_estimator`, 4/11 `alt_control_group`, 6/11 `in_appendix`, 2/11 `in_main_text`, 2/11 `summarized_in_one_table` and 1/11 `summarized_in_figure`. Pointers: restud_2026_restud_rdaf027 p.30-31 Section 5.3; Appendix Figures A12 to A14; aer_111_12_2 p.27-28 Section IIIB, Robustness paragraph; aer_114_7_10 p.30-33 Section VI (alternative control-group constructions). This is the rule with the strongest reported support, and practice goes beyond it: several papers present matching itself as one of several estimators side by side in the main text rather than as an appendix check (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.9-11 Tables 3-5 and Figures 1-2; jfe_2025_j_jfineco_2025_104150 p.11-16 Section 5.1), and one paper builds a matched design specifically to address a spillover threat (jpe_2026_740222 p.44-44 Section VII, Matching difference-in-differences). The sensitivity half of the rule is thinner: 1/11 records `sensitivity_bounds` (aer_113_6_2 p.28-28 Section IV.D, Other Robustness, footnote) and 1/11 records a placebo treatment assignment (aer_114_7_10 p.32-33 Section VI), and the concordance records `robustness` paper_only 8 against agree 1, so the card layer must not be read as evidence that these checks are absent.
- r8 (what the table and the limitations paragraph must state). 3/11 notes record `sample_size_reported_per_table`, 2/11 record `notes_state_sample` under `presentation_convention`, 1/11 `mean_of_dependent_reported` and 3/11 a `limitation_stated` tag of some kind, including 1/11 `identification_caveat` (aer_111_12_2 p.18-18 Section IIIA, footnote). Pointers: rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf080 p.13-13 Section 2.1.8, Tables A.3 and A.4 (an appendix table documenting, per table and figure, the sample restrictions, period, event window and number of firms by group); aer_113_6_2 p.21-21 Table 3, and generally; aer_113_6_2 p.12-13 Table 1. Practice diverges from the rule in that no note records the number of distinct comparison units used under matching with replacement, which is the count that makes the effective sample visible, and only one note records a comparison-group outcome mean in the results table. Practice goes beyond the rule where a paper devotes an appendix table to the per-exhibit sample definition (rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf080 p.13-13 Section 2.1.8, Tables A.3 and A.4), which is a better version of what r8 asks for in table notes.

## Open questions for review

- There is no observed layer for this method. Every code-side statement in this document comes from the concordance block inside `reported.md`, which covers 10 of the 11 notes. A reviewer should decide whether rules that would normally be grounded in code traces (r2 on the procedure, r5 on weights, r6 on the variance estimator) may stay in this document at all before recipe cards carrying `matching` as a design method exist.
- Concordance totals across the 10 notes with a card, summed per status: agree 53, paper_only 59, disagree 5, unknown 4, code_only 3 (124 items). paper_only dominates in `robustness` (8), `data_source` (7), `comparison_group` (6), `outputs` (6), `weights` (6) and `controls` (5). Implication: a null card field for a matching design is a recording gap, not evidence that the step was skipped, and no rule here may be defended by counting card absences. The card fields that can be trusted most in this set are `estimator` (agree 14, disagree 1) and `fixed_effects` (agree 10, disagree 2); the 5 disagreements concern controls, fixed effects and outputs, so a card specification may be a variant rather than the baseline.
- r4 tells authors not to rest balance claims on t-tests or p-values, while the balance evidence recorded in these 11 notes is largely built on exactly those statistics. A reviewer must decide whether the rule is stated too strongly for this literature, or whether the standardised-difference form should be required with the tests reported alongside.
- r6 has almost no reported basis in this method and rests entirely on the matching-inference literature. It also leaves open which variance estimator applies when matching is a preprocessing step and the reported estimate comes from a weighted regression on the matched sample: whether the estimated propensity score may be treated as known, and under which conditions, is a decision the reviewer or the user must make per project rather than a rule this document can fix.
- Whether entropy balancing and other covariate-balancing weights belong in this paradigm or in a separate one. One note records an entropy-balancing construction as the matching step (`variable_construction` `other:balancing_weight_construction` 1/11, aer_111_12_2 p.18-19 Section IIIA), and the applicability section currently keeps it here; the taxonomy owner should confirm.
- Caliper widths, trimming thresholds and standardised-difference thresholds are deliberately not given as numbers in r2, r3 and r4. If the harness wants defaults, they must come from a named source and be recorded as a convention, not inferred from the corpus.
- Rules r1, r5 (statement half) and r8 are reporting and writing rules with no testable artifact. As with the `rdd` draft, a reviewer must decide whether reporting rules belong in this document or in the writer role's conventions.
- No hold-out list exists for `matching`, so no note is excluded from the counts above. If a `matching` skill evaluation is added later, the reported layer will have to be regenerated and these counts revisited.
