# Recommended rules: `fama_macbeth` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (4 validated recipe cards: 3 partial, 1 minimal; design variant unknown in 3 of 4; no card links a specification to an output) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (3 paper-practice notes, all 3 with a linked card; the fourth card, jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746, has no note). The evidence base is thin: with fewer than five papers in either layer, shares are not meaningful and every corpus count below is a pointer, not a frequency. Observed and reported practice are evidence of what authors did, never of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a null card field or an absent tag means "not recorded", not "not done". Each rule states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid + record id + path:lines from `observed.md`; artid + page + locator from `reported.md`; `_all:` prefix for `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`) and the validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; historical build and review records are not distributed.

## Applicability

- Covered: two-pass Fama-MacBeth estimation on stock-period panels in which a return (or excess return) at t is regressed period by period on characteristics observed before t, and the time series of cross-sectional slopes is averaged; monthly, weekly or daily cross-sections; observed characteristics as regressors; the comparison with a pooled panel regression with time fixed effects and double-clustered standard errors. Estimated betas as regressors (the original two-pass design) are covered only by the errors-in-variables exception in r1 and r2.
- Not covered or a different branch: GMM or SDF estimation of factor models; time-series factor regressions and spanning alphas (event-study or `predictive_regression` paradigms); portfolio sorts as the main estimate (a separate paradigm; sorts appear here only as the magnitude reference in r6); Fama-MacBeth applied to non-return outcomes in accounting or corporate finance panels, where the panel companion in r3 is usually the main specification rather than the variant; multiple-testing adjustment across many anomalies beyond the statement required in r6.

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 3 notes in `reported.md`; a note that lacks a tag did not record it. Pointers are artid, page range and locator as printed in `reported.md`; re-read the page before citing. With three notes, agreement or divergence is a pointer to one paper, not a pattern.

- r1 (equation, timing, averaged-slope estimand): 3/3 notes record `design_variant_named` and `estimand_named`; 2/3 `threats_discussed`, 1/3 `identifying_assumption_explicit` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.7-8 Section 4.1, Eq. (8)), 1/3 `identification_caveat` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.5-7 Introduction, alternative explanations paragraph). Pointers: rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.14-19 Section 2, eq. (1) and eq. (2); rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.2-13 Introduction; Section 3 opening paragraph. Practice diverges from the rule in one direction: the papers name the estimand and write the equation, but no note records the timing of characteristics relative to returns as part of the equation statement, so the timing half of r1 has no reported basis and rests on r4's sources. The cards leave the design variant unknown in 3/4, which is a recording gap (the estimator field agrees with the paper in 6 concordance items, disagree 1).
- r2 (Newey-West on the slope series, lag stated and justified): 2/3 notes record `newey_west` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.15-22 Section 2.1; notes to Tables 4 to 9; rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.16-20 footnote 9; Section 3.4), 1/3 `in_footnote`, 1/3 `no_justification_found` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.1-23 throughout, recorded for multiple testing rather than for the lag). Two reader gaps state that the lag length is never given in one paper and never justified beyond citing the correction in another. Practice diverges from the rule in both papers that use Newey-West: the correction is named but the lag rule is not stated, and no note records a lag-sensitivity row. The code layer supplies what the text omits (fixed lags of 8 and 1 in the two Stata calls, an `iid` option in one wrapper), which is the reason r2 asks for the lag in the notes. Across all 91 notes `newey_west` is recorded in only 4 (`_all:` jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.7-9 Tables 3-5 notes), and one paper outside this method argues against classical long-run-variance estimators (`_all:` jpe_2026_742424 p.3-3 Introduction, Figure 2); r2 does not take a position on that critique.
- r3 (panel companion with time fixed effects and firm-and-period clustering): no tag in `reported.md` records a stated comparison between Fama-MacBeth and panel estimates; the evidence is the concordance table (`cluster_level` agree 2: jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 s1 with firm and date clusters; rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 s3 with two-way clustering) and one paper that prints a Fama-MacBeth table and a double-clustered panel table for related outcomes (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.21-33 Table 6; Table 11). Practice diverges from the rule: the panel regressions in these papers answer other questions (volume, volatility, event windows) rather than re-estimating the return equation, and a reader gap records no reasoning for the clustering dimensions. The rule's requirement to say which estimate carries the claim has no reported basis.
- r4 (characteristic timing and per-period winsorization): 3/3 notes record `winsorization` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.6-6 Section 3; rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.19-21 Section 2.3; Table 6 notes; rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.13-45 Table 1 notes; Table 6 notes; Table 11 notes; Table B1 notes), 1/3 `logs_or_ihs`, 1/3 `outcome_definition`, 1/3 `data_public` for a pre-built characteristics dataset (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.11-11 Section 2; footnote 7). Practice goes beyond the rule in one paper, which states different winsorization thresholds for summary statistics, regression controls and a robustness table; practice diverges in that no note records whether percentiles are computed per period or pooled, nor the lag between fiscal-year data and the first return that uses it. No card records winsorization or lagging for a Fama-MacBeth specification.
- r5 (sample screens with counts, stocks per period, microcap variant): 3/3 notes record `exclusions_justified` and `data_source_named`; 2/3 `unit_of_observation_stated` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.6-7 Section 3; rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.11-13 Section 2; Table 1 notes); 2/3 `sample_size_reported_per_table` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.16-22 Tables 4 to 6 and Section 2.3; rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.21-33 Table 6; Table 11); 1/3 `sample_period_stated`; 2/3 `alt_sample`. Practice diverges from the rule: the papers report stock-month counts, not the number of periods and stocks per period, and a reader gap records that the number of stocks per month is absent; no note records a value-weighted or microcap-excluded Fama-MacBeth variant (the weighting observations in the concordance, `weights` agree 1, paper_only 1, unknown 1, concern portfolio sorts, not the cross-sectional regressions). The concordance's `sample` paper_only 3 means the card sample fields cannot be used to check screens.
- r6 (table content and magnitude): 3/3 notes record `n_reported` and `notes_state_se`, 2/3 `r2_reported`, 2/3 `se_type_in_notes`, 2/3 `controls_progressive_columns`, 3/3 `stars_used` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-16 Tables 4-11), 1/3 `sd_units` and `compared_to_literature` (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.16-33 Section 3.1; Section 3.4; Section 5.5), 1/3 `precision_discussed` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-18 throughout Section 4), 1/3 `other:compact_alpha_only_tables`. Practice diverges from the rule in two places: one reader gap records that R-squared and the mean of the dependent variable are missing from a Fama-MacBeth table, and no note records a multiple-testing statement (two reader gaps record its absence; the one card diagnostic is `not_found_in_scope`). Practice goes beyond the rule with progressive-control columns and a swap of the dependent variable across columns (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.19-21 Section 3.4; Table 6).

## Open questions for review

- Evidence base: 4 cards (3 partial, 1 minimal) and 3 notes. Every count above is a pointer; the reviewer should decide whether rules r3, r5 and r6, whose corpus support is one paper or none, should stay as rules or be moved to open items until more cards and notes exist.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 3 notes, 38 items): agree 22, paper_only 13, code_only 1, disagree 1, unknown 1. paper_only is concentrated in robustness (3), sample (3), other (2) and single items for comparison_group, data_source, fixed_effects, se_type and weights. Implication: card fields for sample restrictions, robustness and weights are empty where the papers state them, so `observed.md` must not be read as evidence that screens, variants or weights were absent; the estimator (agree 6, disagree 1) and se_type (agree 4, paper_only 1) fields are the ones that can be trusted. The one disagreement is a design classification (a return event study versus a difference-in-differences label), not a Fama-MacBeth item.
- r2: whether the main inference should be Newey-West by default or the plain Fama-MacBeth standard error with Newey-West as a variant when returns do not overlap; the two Stata calls in the corpus use fixed lags of 8 and 1 at what appears to be monthly frequency, and neither paper states the rule. The reviewer should also decide whether the critique of classical long-run-variance estimators recorded in `_all` (jpe_2026_742424) warrants an alternative (for example a fixed-b or equal-weighted-cosine approach) as an option in r2; no source for that alternative was verified in this pass.
- r2 exception: the Shanken correction applies to estimated betas; whether it is also required when a characteristic is itself an estimate (for example a residual from a first-stage cross-sectional regression, as in jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.7-8 Section 4.1, Eq. (8)) is a generated-regressor question the reviewer must decide.
- r3: whether the panel companion is a required row in the main table or a robustness item; Petersen 2009 and Thompson 2011 were cited from memory of their content, with only the DOI, title and first author verified through Crossref; the specific claim that Fama-MacBeth standard errors are biased with a persistent firm effect should be re-read against Petersen 2009 before the rule leaves draft.
- r4: the July-of-year-t timing convention comes from Fama-French 1992; whether a stated minimum reporting lag (for example four months) is an acceptable alternative for quarterly data, and whether per-period winsorization should be recommended over pooled winsorization, are choices this pass made without a verified source.
- r5: the microcap breakpoint and the choice between excluding microcaps and value-weighted cross-sections are left to the user; Fama-French 2008 and Hou-Xue-Zhang 2020 were verified by DOI only and the specific breakpoints in those papers were not re-read.
- r6: multiple-testing statement rests on Harvey-Liu-Zhu 2016 (DOI verified); the threshold they propose is not restated here because no numerical result from any paper is copied into this file.
- No template exists for this method; validation entries `no template yet` mean that nothing has been tested. A future template should at minimum re-estimate the averaged slope with a stated lag, report the period count and stocks per period, and produce the double-clustered panel companion for comparison.
- Whether reporting rules (r6, parts of r5) belong in this document or in the writer role's conventions, as also raised for `rdd`.
