# Recommended rules: `gmm` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from one generated layer of corpus practice: reported paper practice in `reported.md` (3 paper-practice notes, all 3 with a linked recipe card). There is no observed layer for this method: no recipe card carries `gmm` as its design method, so `observed.md` does not exist and no card field is counted here. The two cards whose specifications use a GMM estimator are distilled under `harness/paradigms/structural_estimation/observed.md` (restud_90_5_11 s6, restud_92_2_7 s2) and are referred to there, not repeated. The evidence base is thin: three notes from two journals, one asset-pricing GMM paper, one simulated-method-of-moments paper and one structural model estimated by moment matching. Every count below is out of 3 unless prefixed `_all:` (91 notes, all methods). These rules therefore rest on the method literature and on judgement; the corpus supplies pointers and shows where practice and rule diverge, never a frequency argument.

## Applicability

- Covered: estimators defined by a finite set of population moment conditions and a quadratic objective (Hansen 1982 sense), with the parameters interpreted through the economic model that supplies the moments: Euler-equation and stochastic-discount-factor tests on asset returns, overidentified instrumental-variables GMM with several instruments, nonlinear GMM on cross-sections or panels, simulated method of moments and indirect inference at the estimator level, and classical minimum distance on first-stage reduced-form estimates.
- Covered only for the estimator side: when the moments come from a solved economic model (solution method, calibration of fixed parameters, counterfactuals, model-fit narrative), the model side belongs to `harness/paradigms/structural_estimation/` and is not repeated here; r6 states the hand-off.
- Not covered / different branch: linear IV with one endogenous regressor and two-stage least squares (`harness/paradigms/iv/`); dynamic panel difference and system GMM, where instrument proliferation and the finite-sample variance correction need rules of their own; moment-inequality and partially identified models (`_all:` restud_90_5_13 p.3-28 Section 1; Section 5.2.5; Section 6.1.2 records the restriction-class dependence of the procedure); Neyman-orthogonal moment conditions with cross-fitting (`_all:` aer_107_5_51 p.1-2 introduction and Section I); Bayesian or likelihood-based estimation of the same models.

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 3 notes in `reported.md` unless prefixed `_all:` (91 notes, all methods). A note that lacks a tag did not record it. Pointers are artid, page range and locator as printed in the generated files; re-read the page before citing.

- r1 (moment conditions and estimand stated): 1/3 notes record `identifying_assumption_explicit` (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.20-21 Section 5.3.1) and 1/3 `estimand_named` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.1-2 Section 1, Introduction); the `design_statement` category has observations in 2/3 papers. No note carries a tag for the moment count or the degree of overidentification, and the readers' tag set has no such tag, so this is a recording gap rather than evidence that papers omit it. Practice diverges from the rule in one direction visible here: the one identification statement recorded is informal (a simplified case), while r1 asks for the explicit moment list and counts.
- r2 (weighting matrix and step choice): 1/3 notes record `other:identity_weighting_matrix_stated` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.10-10 Section 4, GMM setup); it is the only observation in the `inference_justification` category (1/3 papers). No note records a two-step, iterated or continuously updated choice, an efficient-weight variant, or how the long-run covariance of the moments is estimated. Practice as recorded states the fixed-matrix choice but not the alternative that r2 asks for as a variant.
- r3 (overidentification test and moment-level fit): 1/3 notes record `overid_test` (restud_90_5_11 p.31-31 footnote 34, Appendix Table A15), placed `in_appendix` and `in_footnote` (1/3 each); 1/3 record `other:pricing_error_fit_statistics` and `other:fit_statistics_reported` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.11-12 Tables 6-7 notes; jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.11-12 Tables 6-7); 1/3 record `other:model_fit_moments_table` (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.22-23 Table 5, Figure 4, Section 6.1); `in_main_text` 2/3. Across all methods, `_all:` `overid_test` is recorded in 6/91 notes (e.g. `_all:` aer_110_2_5 p.19-46 Appendix Table B2). Practice diverges from the rule in both directions: the one overidentification test recorded is an appendix robustness item rather than a main-table statistic, and the fit tables recorded replace the J statistic rather than accompany it.
- r4 (covariance estimator matched to the sampling scheme, tuning stated): within the 3 notes, no note records a standard-error tag for the GMM estimates themselves; the concordance table has `se_type` agree 1 (a HAC choice stated for spanning regressions), code_only 1, unknown 1 and paper_only 1 (structural standard errors from a firm-level bootstrap stated in table notes, restud_2026_restud_rdag013 d2, with no structural specification in the card). Across all methods, `_all:` `newey_west` 4/91 (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.7-9 Tables 3-5 notes; jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-13 Tables 4-5, 9-11, Figs. 3-6), `_all:` `other:bootstrap_se` 2/91 (qje_140_1_4 p.48-48 Table IV notes), `_all:` `other:newey_west_critiqued` 1/91 (jpe_2026_742424 p.3-3 Introduction, Figure 2). - r5 (identification diagnostics): 1/3 notes record `identifying_assumption_explicit` (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.20-21 Section 5.3.1), and it is an informal exposition; no note among the 3 records a weak-identification statistic, a robust confidence set, a sensitivity measure or a multiple-start check. One reader gap records that the IV robustness column of restud_90_5_11 reports no first-stage or weak-IV statistic. - r6 (simulation design, first-stage covariance, targeted and non-targeted moments): 1/3 notes record `other:multi_step_estimation_described` (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.20-20 Section 5.2) and 1/3 `other:model_fit_moments_table` (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.22-23 Table 5, Figure 4, Section 6.1). ## Open questions for review

- Evidence base: 3 notes (ReStud 2, JFE 1), of which one is an asset-pricing GMM with a fixed weighting matrix, one a simulated method of moments inside a multi-step structural estimation, and one a structural model whose overidentified variant appears only as an appendix check. No observed layer exists; the two card specifications that call a GMM estimator are distilled under `structural_estimation` (restud_90_5_11 s6, restud_92_2_7 s2). The reviewer should decide whether the rules can leave `draft` on this base or whether more GMM notes (asset-pricing Euler-equation tests, overidentified IV-GMM, dynamic panels) must be read first.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 3 notes, 35 items): agree 16, paper_only 13, code_only 3, unknown 2, disagree 1. paper_only is concentrated in diagnostic (4), outputs (2), sample (2), and one each in controls, data_source, robustness, se_type and weights. Implication: the card fields that would matter most for these rules (diagnostics, standard errors of structural estimates, weights) are the ones cards record least, so a null card field must not be read as absence, and no rule here relies on a card count.
- r2 default: whether the harness should recommend the estimated optimal weighting matrix as the baseline with the fixed matrix as a variant, or the reverse for asset-pricing model comparison, where a fixed matrix keeps the objective comparable across models. Options: (a) optimal baseline, fixed variant, J statistic in the main table; (b) fixed baseline with moment-level errors, optimal as a variant with the J statistic. Both are consistent with the sources; the choice depends on whether the paper's claim is about a model's fit or about parameter values, and should go to the user when a plan is written.
- r4 lag rule: whether the harness fixes a convention (a named automatic rule versus a fixed lag stated a priori) or leaves the choice to the plan with a mandatory justification sentence. The reader gaps show that the justification is what is missing in practice.
- r5 for nonlinear and simulated moments: no verified source is cited for the sensitivity-based measure (Andrews, Gentzkow and Shapiro 2017 is unverified here), and the multiple-start check is corpus practice only in spirit; the reviewer should confirm the source or weaken the rule to a reporting requirement.
- r6 placement: whether the simulation-design items belong here or in `harness/paradigms/structural_estimation/recommendations.md`, which is still a skeleton. The current split (estimator side here, model side there) should be confirmed before either file leaves `draft`.
- Source verification: 14 of the 20 sources cited are `unverified`. The Crossref budget for this pass (20 calls) was consumed by 17 calls whose output was lost to a script error, leaving 3 successful verifications; the unverified DOIs are listed below so a reviewer can resolve them with the same procedure.
- Dynamic panel GMM is declared out of scope; a reviewer should decide whether it gets its own paradigm directory or a branch of this file.
