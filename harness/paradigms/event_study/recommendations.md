# Recommended rules: `event_study` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature (see "Method sources") and two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (8 validated recipe cards, 8 designs and 19 specifications; the papers listed in `harness_build/evals/holdout/event_study.json` are excluded) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (30 paper-practice notes, 27 of them with a linked recipe card, after the same paper-level hold-out). The observed layer is thin and should be treated as such: it rests on 8 partial cards, the standard-error type is unknown in 4 of them, the number of clustering dimensions in 5, the fixed-effect count in 6, no card records a design variant, and all 8 lack specification-to-output links, so no rule below can be grounded in a code-level count of event-study figures. Observed and reported frequencies are evidence of practice only, never of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a null card field or an unrecorded note tag means "not recorded", not "not done".

Boundary with `did`. This method shares its identification with difference-in-differences, and the general DiD rules are not repeated here. Estimand and comparison group are `did` r1 and r10; the requirement to use a heterogeneity-robust estimator under staggered adoption and to show it beside TWFE is `did` r2 and r11; the cluster level and its justification are `did` r4 and r12; pre-trend evidence in general and the DiD table and figure notes are `did` r5, r7, r8 and r9; robustness linked to baselines is `did` r6. What follows is only what is specific to the dynamic specification (relative-time indicators, reference period, window, event-time composition, anticipation, aggregation) and to the event-study figure. Where a rule extends a `did` rule, the extension is named in the "applies when" or "exceptions" column.

## Applicability

- Covered: panel or repeated cross-section event studies around treatment timing estimated as dynamic DiD with relative-time indicators; single-date and staggered adoption; imputation, interaction-weighted and group-time estimators aggregated to event time; the event-study figure and its note; sensitivity analysis for violations of parallel trends on the event-time scale.
- Not covered, or a different branch: financial short-horizon event studies around announcement dates with abnormal returns and market-model benchmarks (a different estimand and a different inference problem); designs with treatment reversal or repeated events per unit; continuous or dose-response treatment intensity in event time; distributed-lag models where the regressor is a continuous shock rather than an absorbing indicator; synthetic control with one treated unit.

## Open questions for review

- Whether r5 should require the heterogeneity-robust dynamic path on the same figure as the headline path, or accept it in a companion figure; the corpus records robustness in the appendix in 10/30 notes and offers no example of the shared-figure standard.
- Whether r7's placement requirement (with the main result rather than in a footnote or appendix) is a methodological rule or a writer-role convention; both applied examples in this set place it in a footnote or an appendix.
- Whether simultaneous bands should be the default in r8 or an explicit alternative reported next to pointwise bands; no note in this set records the distinction, so the rule has no corpus grounding and needs a user or reviewer decision.
- Whether r3's per-event-time unit counts are feasible for every estimator (imputation and group-time commands do not all return them) and, if not, what the fallback report is.
- Whether the reference-period and endpoint conventions in r1 and r2 belong here or should be merged into `did` r3, given that `did` r3 already names both without giving a rule; a merge decision also affects whether `fhb distill did --also event_study` should be used for a single paradigm.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 30 notes, 27 with a card): 328 compared items, of which 161 agree, 129 are paper_only, 20 are unknown, 10 disagree and 8 are code_only. paper_only concentrates in robustness (17 against 6 agree), outputs (15 against 2), diagnostic (15 against 16), comparison_group (14 against 2), data_source (14 against 9), fixed_effects (12 against 24) and sample (10 against 13). Implication for trusting card fields: a null or `unknown` card field in these categories is a recording gap, so the observed counts for diagnostics (for example `pretrend_plot:not_found_in_scope` in 2 cards) must not be cited as evidence that a check was skipped, and rules r6, r7 and r10 lean on the reported layer for that reason.
