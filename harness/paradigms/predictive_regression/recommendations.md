# Recommended rules: `predictive_regression` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from one generated layer of corpus practice: reported paper practice in `reported.md` (2 paper-practice notes, both with a linked recipe card). There is no observed layer for this method: no accepted recipe card carries `predictive_regression` as its method, so `observed.md` and `observed.json` do not exist and no code-trace observation is cited below. The evidence base is very thin. Two notes from two finance journals (one JFE, one RFS, both 2026) cannot show what practice looks like; they supply pointers only, and neither note records an out-of-sample evaluation, an in-sample versus out-of-sample split, or a look-ahead statement, so rules r3 and r4 rest on the literature alone. For that reason this pass writes five rules instead of the usual five to ten, and every corpus pointer is a single paper. Reported frequencies are evidence of practice only, never of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a tag absent from a note means "not recorded", not "not done". Every rule states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid + page + locator from `reported.md`, or `_all:` for `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`) and the validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; historical build and review records are not distributed.

## Applicability

- Covered: time-series regressions of future returns (or other future outcomes) at horizon h on predictors dated t or earlier, with one-period or overlapping multi-period outcomes; panel versions with unit and time dimensions (firm-month, country-quarter) where the predictor is lagged relative to the outcome; in-sample coefficient tests and out-of-sample forecast evaluation of the same equation; composite predictors built by a dimension-reduction step (PLS, PCA, combinations) that must be estimated recursively for out-of-sample use.
- Not covered or a different branch: cross-sectional Fama-MacBeth regressions with predictor timing as their main concern (see `fama_macbeth`); portfolio sorts on a lagged characteristic (see `portfolio_sorts`); machine-learning return prediction with many predictors and tuned models (see `ml_prediction`); event-time abnormal returns (see `return_event_study`); vector autoregressions and variance decompositions of returns, which share the persistence problem but need their own estimand statement.

## Rules

| id | rule | applies when | exceptions | sources (verified?) | corpus pointers | validation |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| r1 | Before interpreting a coefficient on a persistent predictor, report the predictor's estimated first-order autocorrelation and the correlation between predictor innovations and outcome innovations on the estimation sample; when the predictor is near-persistent and that correlation is far from zero, report a bias-corrected estimate or a test robust to persistence (Bonferroni Q-test, IVX) next to the OLS estimate, and say which one the claim rests on. | time-series predictive regressions with valuation ratios, yields, or other slowly moving predictors; short panels where the time dimension carries the identification | predictors that are approximately serially uncorrelated (past returns, surprises); panels where the number of units is large and the claim is about the cross-sectional slope, in which case state that the persistence problem is second-order and why | Stambaugh 1999 (verified_online); Campbell and Yogo 2006 (verified_online); Kostakis, Magdalinos and Stamatogiannis 2015 (verified_online) | no note among the 2 records a persistence diagnostic; the closest is the estimand statement in jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.4-8 Section 2, Propositions 1-2 and the measurement caveat in jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.10-10 Section 4.1 | no template yet |
| r2 | For overlapping h-period outcomes, state the HAC rule before estimation and tie it to h: a fixed lag of at least h minus 1 with the Newey-West (Bartlett) or Hansen-Hodrick weighting, or an automatic rule with its citation; report the lag used in the table notes; report the one-period regression alongside any long-horizon column and treat long-horizon t-statistics as size-distorted unless a method robust to the overlap is used; show sensitivity to nearby lag choices as a robustness entry, never as the main choice. | any horizon h greater than one at the sampling frequency; also one-period regressions when residual autocorrelation is plausible | non-overlapping sampling of the h-period outcome (state the loss of observations); panels where cross-sectional dependence dominates, which use r5 | Newey and West 1987 (verified_online); Newey and West 1994 (verified_online); Hansen and Hodrick 1980 (verified_online); Hodrick 1992 (verified_online); Boudoukh, Richardson and Whitelaw 2008 (verified_online); Valkanov 2003 (unverified); Lazarus, Lewis, Stock and Watson 2018 (verified_online) | 1/2 notes record `newey_west`: jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-13 Tables 4-5, 9-11, Figs. 3-6 (automatic lag); a reader gap on the same paper records no justification for the lag length; cross-method: _all: jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.7-9 Tables 3-5 notes; _all: jpe_2026_742424 p.3-3 Introduction, Figure 2 (a paper that argues against classical long-run variance estimators) | no template yet |
| r3 | When an out-of-sample claim is made, state the forecast benchmark (the recursive historical mean for a single return series; a zero or unconditional-mean forecast for a panel of returns, with the choice stated), the out-of-sample R-squared formula relative to that benchmark, the estimation window (expanding or rolling, with its length), the first forecast date and the reason it was fixed before the results were seen; report a nested-model forecast comparison test and a cumulative squared-error difference plot over the evaluation period so that the reader can see whether the gain comes from a few dates. | every out-of-sample statement, including those in an appendix | purely in-sample papers: state that no out-of-sample evidence is claimed; the in-sample versus out-of-sample choice is a statement about the question (population predictability versus real-time usefulness), not a substitute for either | Welch and Goyal 2008 (verified_online); Campbell and Thompson 2008 (verified_online); Clark and West 2007 (verified_online); Rapach, Strauss and Zhou 2010 (verified_online); Gu, Kelly and Xiu 2020 (verified_online; zero benchmark for panel out-of-sample R-squared); Inoue and Kilian 2005 (verified_online) | no note among the 2 records an out-of-sample tag, a split date or a benchmark; the only R-squared reporting recorded is in-sample: rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.32-33 Section 5.5; Equation (5); Table 11 (`r2_reported`), and a reader gap records missing R-squared for Fama-MacBeth tables in the same paper | no template yet |
| r4 | Build every predictor dated t from information available at t under the stated information set: lag accounting and macro series by their release dates, avoid full-sample standardisation, demeaning or scaling of the predictor when the claim is out-of-sample, and re-estimate any dimension-reduction step (PLS, PCA, forecast combination weights) recursively inside the out-of-sample loop; document the information set and the alignment of predictor and outcome dates in the data section and in the table notes. | always; most important for composite predictors and for outcomes measured over windows that begin after the predictor date | in-sample descriptive regressions may use full-sample standardisation if the text says so and the coefficient is interpreted in standard-deviation units of the full-sample predictor | Welch and Goyal 2008 (verified_online; real-time construction of predictors); Campbell and Thompson 2008 (verified_online; forecasts use data through t only); corpus practice only for the PLS case | 1/2 notes record a composite predictor built by a two-step PLS procedure: jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.12-13 Section 4.2.2 (`other:pls_composite_predictor`); whether that step was estimated recursively is not recorded in the note; 1/2 notes record `sample_period_stated` and `unit_of_observation_stated`: jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.6-7 Section 3; standard-deviation interpretation in jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-16 throughout Section 4 (`sd_units`) | no template yet |
| r5 | In panel predictive regressions, choose the inference from the dependence structure and say why: cluster by time (or use Driscoll-Kraay with a stated lag) when a common shock across units in a period is plausible, add the unit dimension when the residual is persistent within unit, and report the number of periods and units in the table notes next to the SE type; when the number of periods is small, state that time clustering rests on few clusters and give an alternative; when many predictors or many horizons are tested, say how the number of tests was handled. The main table reports N, R-squared (within R-squared when fixed effects are absorbed), and the mean or standard deviation of the outcome, and its notes state the SE type, cluster level, fixed effects and controls. | panel predictive regressions (firm-month, fund-quarter, country-year); also time-series regressions run on several test assets at once | a single time series with one unit: r2 applies; a Fama-MacBeth alternative belongs to `fama_macbeth` and must not be mixed with panel-clustered columns in one table without a stated reason | Petersen 2009 (verified_online); Thompson 2011 (verified_online); Driscoll and Kraay 1998 (verified_online); Harvey, Liu and Zhu 2016 (verified_online; multiple testing in return prediction) | 1/2 notes record `cluster_level_in_notes`, `fe_listed_in_table`, `n_reported`, `r2_reported`: rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.32-33 Section 5.5; Equation (5); Table 11; 1/2 record `no_justification_found`: jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.1-23 throughout (multiple testing not discussed); reader gaps record no reasoning for the clustering dimensions and no multiple-testing adjustment in both papers; cross-method: _all: jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.15-15 Table 6 notes (Driscoll-Kraay with a stated maximum lag) | no template yet |

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 2 notes in `reported.md`; a note that lacks a tag did not record it. Pointers are artid, page range and locator as printed in `reported.md` (or in `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md` with the prefix `_all:`); re-read the page before citing. With two notes, every count below is 0, 1 or 2, and no count says anything about how common a practice is.

- r1 (persistence diagnostic and robust test): 0/2 notes record any persistence or Stambaugh-type diagnostic; the `diagnostic_reported` category is present in 1/2 and contains a control-validity check (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.12-13 Section 4.2.2, Table 5) and an appendix time-series plot of the shock variables (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.16-17 Section 4.5, Appendix Fig. A.1 referenced). 1/2 record `estimand_named` and `design_variant_named` from a theoretical model (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.4-8 Section 2, Propositions 1-2). Practice as recorded diverges from the rule: neither paper is recorded as reporting the predictor's autocorrelation or a bias-corrected estimate. Whether this reflects the papers or the readers' tag set cannot be decided from two notes.
- r2 (HAC lag tied to the horizon, lag reported, long-horizon caution): 1/2 notes record `newey_west` with an automatic lag (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-13 Tables 4-5, 9-11, Figs. 3-6); the reader gap "No justification given for the Newey-West lag length beyond citing the correction" is the divergence the rule addresses. Across all 91 notes, `_all:` records `newey_west` in 4 papers (e.g. _all: jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.7-9 Tables 3-5 notes) and one methodological paper that argues against classical long-run variance estimators (_all: jpe_2026_742424 p.3-3 Introduction, Figure 2), so the corpus contains both the practice and a critique of it. No note records a long-horizon regression or a non-overlapping alternative.
- r3 (out-of-sample benchmark, split and test): 0/2 notes record an out-of-sample evaluation, a split date or a benchmark; the reported layer has no tag for these. The in-sample R-squared is recorded in 1/2 (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.32-33 Section 5.5; Equation (5); Table 11) and a reader gap records "R-squared and mean of the dependent variable not reported for Fama-MacBeth tables" in the same paper. The rule therefore has no corpus support in either direction and rests on the method literature.
- r4 (no look-ahead in predictor construction): 1/2 notes record a composite predictor built by PLS (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.12-13 Section 4.2.2); the note does not record whether the PLS weights were estimated on the full sample or recursively, which is exactly the information the rule requires. 1/2 record `sample_period_stated`, `unit_of_observation_stated` and `data_source_named` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.6-7 Section 3) and `sd_units` interpretation (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-16 throughout Section 4), which under the exception in r4 requires the standardisation sample to be stated. Practice as recorded neither confirms nor contradicts the rule.
- r5 (panel inference stated and justified; table content): 1/2 notes record `cluster_level_in_notes`, `fe_listed_in_table`, `n_reported` and `r2_reported` (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.32-33 Section 5.5; Equation (5); Table 11); 1/2 record `notes_state_se` and `notes_state_sample` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.7-20 throughout, e.g. Tables 1, 4, 7). The concordance table shows the cluster level agreeing between paper and card in both papers (cluster_level agree 2), so the table-notes half of the rule is followed. The justification half diverges: reader gaps record "No reasoning offered for the clustering dimensions in panel regressions (month and firm; day)" and no multiple-testing adjustment in both papers, and 1/2 record `no_justification_found` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.1-23 throughout). Driscoll-Kraay with a stated maximum lag appears once across the whole corpus (_all: jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.15-15 Table 6 notes), in a difference-in-differences table rather than a predictive regression.

## Open questions for review

- Evidence base: 2 notes, 0 cards, 0 out-of-sample observations. The reviewer should decide whether a rules document can leave draft on this base at all, or whether it must wait for recipe cards with `predictive_regression` as method (candidates: the return-prediction papers already in the corpus whose cards are filed under `fama_macbeth`, `portfolio_sorts` or `regression`) and for paper-practice notes that carry out-of-sample tags.
- Tag coverage: the reported layer has no tag for out-of-sample evaluation, split date, benchmark, persistence diagnostic or look-ahead. Rules r1, r3 and r4 cannot be checked against the corpus until the note schema records these; the reviewer should say whether to add tags or keep them as free-text `other:` entries.
- r1: whether the harness recommends one robust procedure (Bonferroni Q-test, IVX, or a bias correction) as the default or leaves the choice open; the three sources differ in scope (univariate versus multivariate predictors) and none was re-read in this pass beyond the abstract-level claim, so the mapping of each to a design is `unverified`.
- r2: whether "at least h minus 1 lags" is stated as a floor or the automatic rule of Newey and West 1994 is the default; and whether long-horizon columns should be discouraged rather than caveated. Lazarus et al. 2018 recommend fixed-b critical values with a larger bandwidth, which is not the same as the Newey-West rule; the reviewer must decide which of the two the harness adopts as the main choice and which as the sensitivity.
- r3: the benchmark for panel out-of-sample R-squared (zero versus unconditional mean versus historical mean) changes the sign of the statistic; the rule only requires that the choice be stated. The reviewer may want a stronger rule.
- r5: whether a few-periods threshold for time clustering is written as a number (harness convention) or left qualitative; whether the multiple-testing sentence belongs here or in a cross-method inference rule.
- Valkanov 2003 could not be resolved through Crossref in this pass (the DOI query returned no record within the 20-call budget) and remains `unverified`; the reviewer should resolve it or drop it from r2.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 2 notes, 27 items): agree 15, paper_only 10, unknown 1, disagree 1, code_only 0. paper_only is concentrated in sample (2), robustness (2), other (2), comparison_group (1), data_source (1), se_type (1) and weights (1). Implication: for these two cards the estimator (agree 4, disagree 1), fixed effects (agree 2), controls (agree 2), cluster level (agree 2) and se_type (agree 3) fields can be trusted; the empty sample-restriction, robustness and weights fields are recording gaps and must not be read as absence. The single disagreement is a design classification (a test named difference-in-differences in the paper and filed as a return event study on the card), which is a card-method question and does not affect any rule here.
- Both notes are from 2026 finance journals; nothing in this document speaks to predictive regressions in economics journals (exchange rates, inflation, macro forecasting), where the same rules apply but the corpus has no note.
