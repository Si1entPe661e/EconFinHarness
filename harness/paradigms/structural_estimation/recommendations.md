# Recommended rules: `structural_estimation` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (4 validated recipe cards, 4 designs, 7 specifications, all four cards with `coverage: partial`) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (14 paper-practice notes read from the paper text, 12 of them with a linked recipe card; no hold-out list exists for this method, so nothing is excluded). The evidence base is thin on the code side: with 4 cards the shares printed in `observed.md` are not meaningful and are used here only as pointers, the design variant is unknown in 2 of 4 cards, the standard-error type in 2 of 4, and the diagnostics facet rests on a single card with status `unknown`. The paper layer is larger but still 14 notes, and many structural-specific practices appear as one-off `other:` tags rather than as shared vocabulary. Observed and reported frequencies are evidence of practice only, never of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a tag absent from a note or a null card field means "not recorded", not "not done". Every rule below states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid plus record id for `observed.md`, artid plus page and locator for `reported.md`, prefix `_all:` for `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`) and its validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; historical build and review records are not distributed.

## Applicability

- Covered: parametric economic models whose parameters are recovered from data by maximum likelihood, (simulated) method of moments or GMM, indirect inference, or a calibration-plus-estimation split; static and dynamic discrete choice, auction and demand models, dynamic programming models solved numerically, and DSGE-style models estimated by Bayesian or classical methods, including multi-step pipelines whose first stage is a reduced-form regression or hazard model.
- Not covered: purely reduced-form designs (`rdd`, `did`, `iv`, `bunching` have their own paradigm directories), sufficient-statistics welfare arguments that avoid estimating a full model (these appear alongside structural estimates in the corpus but are a different branch), non-parametric or partial-identification set estimation, and machine-learning surrogate models used for prediction rather than for policy counterfactuals.
- Boundary case: papers that estimate a structural model only to interpret a reduced-form estimate. Rules r1, r2, r5 and r9 apply to the structural part; the reduced-form part follows its own method paradigm.

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 14 notes in `reported.md`; a note lacking a tag did not record the practice, which is not the same as the paper omitting it. Pointers are artid, page range and locator exactly as printed in `reported.md`; re-read the page before citing.

- r1 (identification argument per parameter): 4/14 notes record `identifying_assumption_explicit`, 4/14 `estimand_named`, 5/14 `design_variant_named`, 4/14 `threats_discussed`, 1/14 `identification_caveat`, 1/14 `instrument_exclusion_argued`. Pointers: jpe_2025_735509 p.27-33 Section IV, Assumptions 1-6; aer_112_10_8 p.9-23 Section I.B footnote; Section III.C Fact 3; restud_90_2_4 p.25-26 Section 6.4, footnote 29. Divergence: the numbered-assumption style of jpe_2025_735509 goes further than the rule by flagging where an assumption should not be read causally (jpe_2025_735509 p.29-29 Section IV.A, Assumption 4 discussion), while no note in this set records a moment-sensitivity measure of the kind the rule asks for, so the sensitivity half of r1 has no corpus support here and rests on the method source alone.
- r2 (calibrated versus estimated, separately listed): the practice appears only as one-off tags, 1/14 each for `other:calibration_from_statute` (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.19-20 Sections 5.1.4, 5.2 and footnote 19), `other:calibration_within_estimation` (restud_2025_restud_rdae072 p.30-30 Section 6.2), `other:variable_glossary_table` (aer_112_10_8 p.10-10 Table 1) and `other:calibration_grid` (restud_90_5_11 p.28-31 Section 7.2, Table 5, footnote 30). Divergence: papers more often treat calibration as a robustness or limitation item than as a table structure, and one note records the calibration assumption only as a caveat (qje_140_1_4 p.47-47 Section IV.A, footnote); the rule asks for it in the main parameter table.
- r3 (moment fit table and overidentification): `diagnostic_reported` is present in 10/14 notes, but the moment-fit forms are all singletons, `other:model_fit_moments_table`, `other:moment_matching_table`, `other:model_fit`, `other:model_fit_summary` and `overid_test` at 1/14 each. Pointers: restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.22-23 Table 5, Figure 4, Section 6.1; restud_2025_restud_rdae072 p.31-32 Section 6.4, Table 9; restud_90_5_11 p.31-31 footnote 34, Appendix Table A15. Divergence: papers frequently show fit as an overlay figure rather than a moment table (aer_109_7_8 p.35-36 Figures 5 and 6, `other:model_fit_plot`), and one note reports overidentification only after adding calibrated parameters as a robustness check, not as a headline test; the rule's "one table" form is more prescriptive than observed practice.
- r4 (untargeted moment validation): 1/14 notes record `other:non_targeted_moment_check` (qje_140_1_4 p.53-55 Section IV.B.2, Figures VI-VIII), 1/14 `other:model_fit_by_subgroup` (restud_90_5_11 p.26-26 Section 6.4, footnote 29), 1/14 `other:model_fit_check` (jpe_2025_735509 p.37-38 Figure 4) and 1/14 `other:likelihood_ratio_test` (restud_90_2_4 p.11-11 Section 4, Table 3 discussion). Divergence: the validation is usually placed in an appendix (`in_appendix` 5/14, e.g. jpe_2025_735509 p.41-41 Section V.D; online appendix table) rather than next to the estimates, and no note states explicitly which reported moments were targets, which is the part of the rule with no corpus precedent.
- r5 (standard errors and their assumptions): the category is recorded in 7/14 notes; `no_justification_found` 3/14, and the bootstrap variants are one note each (`other:bootstrap_reestimation`, `other:bootstrap_at_firm_level`, `other:parametric_bootstrap`, `other:block_bootstrap`, `other:bootstrap_se`), with `other:bayesian_posterior` and `other:varhac_se_for_correlations` at 1/14. Divergence: papers go beyond the rule by benchmarking a structural number two independent ways and by stating when the model cannot rationalize a finding, and one note flags its welfare formula as first order and envelope-theorem based (restud_90_5_11 p.12-12 Section 2.4 after Theorem 1), a limitation class the rule names only generically.

## Open questions for review

- Concordance between paper statements and the recipe cards (`reported.md`, 14 notes, 142 items): agree 53, paper_only 65, unknown 11, disagree 7, code_only 6. `paper_only` concentrates in diagnostic (10 of 15 items), sample (8 of 11), outputs (8 of 12), data_source (7 of 11) and robustness (7 of 8). Implication for this document: card fields for diagnostics, robustness, samples and outputs must not be cited as evidence that a check was skipped, and every "no card records this" phrase in the rules above is a statement about the cards, not about the papers. The estimator field is the most trustworthy (agree 14, disagree 0, paper_only 4, code_only 2); se_type is split (agree 2, unknown 2, paper_only 2, code_only 1) and should be checked against the paper before use.
- The 7 disagreements sit in fixed effects (2), outputs (3), diagnostics (1) and sample framing (1), none of them in a structural specification. A reviewer should decide whether the structural parts of the cards are simply not extracted (all four cards are `coverage: partial`) rather than in agreement.
- Rule scope: r1's sensitivity requirement (Andrews, Gentzkow and Shapiro 2017) is a real cost for large dynamic models. A reviewer or the user must decide whether it is required for every paper, required only when a counterfactual is reported, or downgraded to "state the identifying variation".
- Rule r5 leaves open which two-step variance route to prefer when the first step is a hazard or a hedonic regression estimated on a different sample; the corpus shows whole-pipeline bootstrapping in one note and an iid first-stage standard error in one card, and no source in this list settles the sample-overlap case.
- Rule r6 asks for uncertainty around counterfactuals; for calibrated parameters there is no standard error by construction. Whether to require a sensitivity range instead, and how wide, is unresolved and needs a user decision.
- No template and no eval exists for this method, so every validation cell is `no template yet` or `not testable by a template`. A reviewer should decide whether a minimal template (parameter table with calibrated and estimated blocks, moment fit table, bootstrap driver with seed) is worth building before this file leaves draft.
- The observed layer is 4 partial cards from 3 journals and 2 years. A reviewer should decide whether that is enough to keep the observed pointers in this document at all, or whether the rules should cite the reported layer only until more cards exist.
- Judd 1998 is cited from memory for the numerical-settings rule and is unverified (no DOI checked in this pass). Either verify it or drop it and rely on Aruoba, Fernandez-Villaverde and Rubio-Ramirez 2006.
- Significance stars: `no_stars` 4/14 against `stars_used` 1/14, too thin to write a rule; the writer role's convention decides.
