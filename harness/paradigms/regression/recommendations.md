# Recommended rules: `regression` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (6 validated recipe cards, all with partial coverage, 13 specifications; the two held-out cards listed in `harness/paradigms/regression/reported.md` are excluded) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (24 paper-practice notes taken from the paper text, of which 20 have a linked recipe card; the same two held-out papers are excluded and count only in `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`). The evidence base is thin on the code side: only 2 of 6 cards record a standard-error type, 1 of 6 records a table-export call, 5 of 6 have no specification-to-output link, and no design variant is recorded, so no variant-conditional rule can be grounded in the cards. The paper layer is broader but heterogeneous: the `regression` label is a residual category that covers descriptive and conditional-correlation regressions inside papers whose headline design may be an experiment, a bunching estimator or a structural model, so tag counts below describe the regression tables of those papers, not their headline designs. Observed and reported frequencies are evidence of practice only, not of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a tag absent from a note or a null card field means "not recorded", not "not done". Every rule states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid + record id from `observed.md`; artid + page + locator from `reported.md`; `_all:` prefix for `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`) and the validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; historical build and review records are not distributed.

## Applicability

- Covered: regressions whose evidence is a conditional association or a treatment-control contrast without a named quasi-experimental design: OLS with or without high-dimensional fixed effects on cross-section or panel data; Poisson or PPML for counts and non-negative outcomes with zeros; linear probability, logit and probit models for binary outcomes; descriptive regressions that document a fact, select controls, or check a mechanism inside a paper whose headline design is something else; reduced-form regressions that a structural model is later asked to match.
- Not covered or a different branch: difference-in-differences and event studies (`did`, `event_study`), instrumental variables and shift-share (`iv`, `shift_share`), regression discontinuity (`rdd`), matching (`matching`), predictive and asset-pricing regressions including Fama-MacBeth (`predictive_regression`), structural estimation. The observed layer contains two cards whose recorded specifications carry leads and lags or a treatment-by-post interaction (restud_2025_restud_rdaf104 s2, restud_2026_restud_rdaf027 s1); rules for those specifications belong to the `did` paradigm and this file cites them only for inference and fixed-effect practice.

## Open questions for review

- Scope of the label: the `regression` cards and notes are a residual category. Two of the 6 cards record leads and lags or a treatment-by-post interaction (restud_2025_restud_rdaf104 s2, restud_2026_restud_rdaf027 s1), and several notes come from papers whose headline design is an experiment or a bunching estimator. The reviewer should decide whether such cards are re-labelled to `did` or `event_study` before the next distillation, and whether the reported layer should be restricted to notes where the regression is the main evidence.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 24 notes, 248 items): agree 115, paper_only 103, unknown 17, code_only 7, disagree 6. paper_only concentrates in diagnostic (16), robustness (15), outputs (13), sample (12), fixed_effects (9), data_source (8), cluster_level (7) and comparison_group (6). Implication: a null card field for a sample, a robustness entry, a fixed effect or a cluster level is a recording gap, and `observed.md` must not be cited as evidence that a paper skipped a check or left errors unclustered. The estimator field (agree 24, disagree 0, code_only 2) and weights (agree 10) are the card fields that can be trusted most.
- The 6 disagreements concern a weak-IV diagnostic (jpe_2026_738249 x3), fixed effects in one PPML specification (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 s1), output counts in two cards and two SE entries (restud_2025_restud_rdaf104 s1 records analytic clustering where the paper states a cluster bootstrap; rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf079 s1 records a cluster with a null level). Implication for r5: the card SE field can differ from the paper's stated procedure in the resampling dimension, so the option-to-call trace in `inference-clustering` step 13 is needed before a card is cited as evidence of the SE actually reported.
- The 7 code_only items include two estimators the text never names (a Fama-MacBeth two-step in jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 s1, PPML in restud_2026_restud_rdag013 s1) and a clustered SE the text does not state (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 d4). r4 and r6 ask for the estimator row and the count model to be named; the reviewer should decide whether the rule should also require the text to name the estimator when the table notes do.
- r6 recommends Poisson over log(1+y) as the main specification for outcomes with zeros. This is a method-literature position, not a corpus one (the corpus shows both in the same paper). The reviewer should decide whether the rule is stated as a requirement or as a reporting requirement (report both, state which is headline).
- r7's incidental-parameters statement is from the author's knowledge and is unverified; a source should be added or the clause dropped.
- r2's selection-on-unobservables bound (Oster 2019) is a recommendation with known sensitivity to the assumed R-squared maximum; the reviewer should decide whether the harness fixes a default or leaves it to the plan as an `unresolved` item.
- Significance stars split the notes (stars_used 5/24, no_stars 5/24) and no rule prescribes them; the writer role's convention decides.
- Rules r3, r4, r9 and r10 are reporting and interpretation rules taken from paper practice; they carry no method source for the convention itself and are not testable by a template. They need a reviewer decision on whether reporting rules belong here or in the writer role's conventions.
- No template exists for this paradigm. r2, r5, r6, r7, r8 and r9 name the checks a template would run; none has been executed, and the `validation` column must not be read as a test result.
- Placement tags (`in_main_text`, `in_appendix`, `in_footnote`) are reader judgements about the paper text and cannot be validated against code.
