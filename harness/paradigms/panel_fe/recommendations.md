# Recommended rules: `panel_fe` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (7 validated recipe cards, 24 specifications, all 7 cards marked `partial` coverage; the held-out cards aer_113_3_2 and rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf081 are excluded there) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (19 paper-practice notes taken from the paper text, all 19 with a linked recipe card, same paper-level hold-out). The observed layer is thin: 7 papers, all Stata, with standard-error type unknown in 2 of 7, cluster dimensions unknown in 4 of 7, weights unknown in 6 of 7, and no design variant recorded at all, so no rule below is grounded in a code-level frequency alone and no variant-conditional rule can be derived from the cards. Observed and reported frequencies are evidence of practice only, never of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a tag absent from a note or a null card field means "not recorded", not "not done". Every rule states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid + record id from `observed.md`; artid + page + locator from `reported.md`, or with an `_all:` prefix from `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md`) and the validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; historical build and review records are not distributed.

## Applicability

- Covered: panel or repeated cross-section regressions in which unit, time and higher-dimensional fixed effects are the main identification device and there is no named quasi-experiment (no cutoff, no instrument, no discrete policy event that the design is built around). Continuous, ordinal or multi-valued key regressors. Additive and interacted fixed effects, absorbed by a high-dimensional estimator or entered as dummies. Linear models and fixed-effect Poisson or logit variants of the same design. Cross-sectional dependence, spatial dependence and serial dependence in such panels.
- Not covered, different branch: difference-in-differences and event studies with a dated treatment (use `paradigms/did` and `paradigms/event_study`, and rule r9 here only as a referral), instrumental variables (`paradigms/iv`), regression discontinuity and kink designs (`paradigms/rdd`), synthetic control, matching, and structural or calibrated models. Random-effects and correlated random-effects estimators, interactive fixed effects and factor models, and nonlinear panel models with incidental-parameter corrections beyond Poisson and logit are out of scope for this pass.

| r7 | If the outcome has a mass of exact zeros, do not report a log-transformed coefficient as a percentage effect. Report the share of zeros in the estimation sample first. | r8 | Build every lag, lead and difference from a declared panel index, never from row order, and state the assumed information set: a regressor dated t must be predetermined with respect to the outcome shock at t under that information set. | r9 | If the key regressor is a binary indicator that switches on at different dates for different units, this is not a plain panel-FE design: the two-way fixed-effect coefficient is a weighted average of unit-and-period-specific effects whose weights can be negative under effect heterogeneity. | r10 | The main panel-FE table reports, per column: the coefficient, the standard error or confidence interval, N, the number of groups in each absorbed dimension, and the mean of the dependent variable on the estimation sample. ## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 19 notes in `reported.md`; a note that lacks a tag did not record it, and a category is only present in a subset of notes (for example `inference_justification` in 11/19, `specification_reporting` in 10/19), so a count of 7/19 can still be a majority of the notes that addressed the item. Pointers are artid, page range and locator as printed in `reported.md`; `_all:` pointers come from `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md` (91 notes). Re-read the page before citing.

- r1 (estimand and identifying variation): 7/19 notes record `threats_discussed`, 5/19 `estimand_named`, 4/19 `identifying_assumption_explicit`, 4/19 `design_variant_named`, and 1/19 `identification_caveat`. Pointers: aer_110_5_5 p.14-15 Section III, opening paragraphs; restud_2025_restud_rdae072 p.9-10 Section 3.1, equations (1)-(2); aer_113_1_1 p.23-23 Section V.A. Practice diverges from the rule in both directions. It falls short in that the `design_statement` category is present in only 9/19 notes at all, so half the notes record no design paragraph of any kind, and the estimand is named less often than threats are discussed. It goes beyond the rule where papers state plainly that a specification is not causal and label it as such (aer_113_1_1 p.23-23 Section V.A; restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.12-12 Section 3.3), which r1 asks for but many notes do not record. `limitation_stated` carries `interpretation_caveat` 6/19 and `identification_caveat` 4/19, which is where this material appears when it is not in the design section.
- r2 (fixed-effect structure chosen from the confounding structure, reported across structures): 2/19 notes record `alt_fixed_effects` and 3/19 `controls_progressive_columns`; 8/19 record `fe_listed_in_table`. Pointers: aer_110_5_5 p.31-31 footnote 38 (a range of area fixed-effect structures); aer_110_5_5 p.17-18 Table 2, panel A1 (columns progressing from a minimal fixed-effects model to the benchmark); restud_2025_restud_rdaf104 p.27-28 Section 5.3; Appendix Tables C8, C13, C14; Appendix Figure C26. Practice diverges from the rule in that the progression is usually presented as a robustness range rather than as a choice justified by a named confounder, and in that the alternative structures are placed in footnotes and appendices (`robustness_reported` `in_appendix` 6/19, `in_footnote` 2/19) rather than next to the baseline. No note records a stated rationale for why one interacted dimension was chosen over two additive ones.
- r3 (post-absorption sample, singletons, nesting, versions): 5/19 notes record `n_reported` and 6/19 `sample_size_reported_per_table`; no note records a singleton count, an absorbed-level count or a degrees-of-freedom statement. Pointers: aer_110_5_5 p.17-18 Table 2 notes (observations and cells reported for every specification); aer_113_1_1 p.10-17 Table 2; Table 4 (counts vary across specifications). The rule is well ahead of reported practice here. The software side is worse: four of the thirty reader gaps in `reported.md` record a paper whose main text never names the estimation software or package, two notes carry an explicit `other:software_not_named` tag, and `software_named` is 4/19, so the version-dependent singleton and degrees-of-freedom behaviour that r3 asks to record is not recoverable from the paper text in most of this set.
- r4 (cluster level from the design, justified in the text): `no_justification_found` is the most frequent tag in the `inference_justification` category at 7/19, against `cluster_at_unit_level` 4/19 and `cluster_at_assignment_level` 2/19; the placement tags are `in_main_text` 3/19, `in_footnote` 3/19 and `in_appendix` 3/19. Pointers: aer_113_1_1 p.10-10 Table 2 notes and aer_115_1_8 p.12-24 Tables 2, 3, 6 notes both state the level in the notes and give no textual reason; ecta_92_2_2 p.13-13 Section 4.1, baseline specification paragraph and restud_2025_restud_rdaf104 p.23-23 Section 5.1 do give one. The divergence is explicit: naming the level in table notes is common (`cluster_level_in_notes` 7/19), justifying the choice in the text is not, and the same pattern holds across all methods (`_all:` `no_justification_found` 57/91 against `cluster_at_assignment_level` 35/91, e.g. `_all:` aer_107_1_5 p.18-18 Section III.A). r4 asks for more than the corpus supplies.
- r5 (cluster counts, two dimensions, few clusters): 2/19 notes record `twoway_cluster` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.14-14 Table 7, footnote 23; rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.33-39 Section 5.5; Section 6.1; Table 14 notes), 1/19 `few_clusters_addressed` and 1/19 `alt_se`, both in the same footnote (rfs_2025_rfs_hhaf065 p.16-16 Footnote 15), and 2/19 `randomization_inference` (ecta_92_2_2 p.14-14 Table I and subsequent result tables; restud_2025_restud_rdaf104 p.19-28 Section 4.3; Section 5.3 Non-random exposure; Appendix Figures C17, C25; footnote 28). No note in this set records the number of clusters in any dimension. This is the rule's largest gap against practice: the corpus supplies the reporting of the method but not of the counts on which the method's validity depends. Papers also go beyond the rule by reporting randomization-inference p-values next to cluster-robust standard errors as a standing convention rather than as a few-cluster remedy, which r5 treats as an alternative rather than a complement.
- r6 (spatial and pervasive cross-sectional dependence): 2/19 notes record `spatial_hac` (aer_110_5_5 p.15-16 Section III, which also states robustness to alternative spatial cutoffs; restud_2025_restud_rdaf104 p.23-23 Section 5.1) and the `in_appendix` placement tag 3/19. The cutoff sensitivity the rule asks for is recorded once, in the appendix (aer_110_5_5 p.16-16 Section III). Across all methods the practice is comparable (`_all:` `spatial_hac` 10/91, e.g. `_all:` aer_110_2_5 p.14-14 Section II.B, footnote 23). Practice diverges from the rule in that the alternative appears as a robustness row rather than as the baseline choice (`_all:` aer_113_3_7 p.28-28 Section III.C, discussion of Online Appendix Table C10 relegates a coarser-level check to an online appendix), and no note records the number of periods when a long-T estimator is used.
- r7 (zeros, logs and count outcomes): 4/19 notes record `logs_or_ihs`, e.g. aer_110_5_5 p.17-18 Table 2 notes (counts log-transformed after adding a small constant, to accommodate zeros) and qje_140_1_11 p.32-33 Section V, Figure V notes (inverse hyperbolic sine of counts). 7/19 record `outcome_definition`. The divergence is direct: the notes record the transformation and the constant but not the share of zeros, and no note records the two-constant sensitivity that r7 asks for; one paper reports the nonlinear count estimator only as a footnoted robustness check (aer_110_5_5 p.18-18 footnote 23, `alt_estimator` 1/19) rather than as the main specification. On the code side, one card uses PPML as the main estimator (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 s1), which is closer to the rule than the paper layer suggests, and that paper reports a log-likelihood instead of an R-squared (restud_2026_restud_rdag013 p.10-11 Table 2 and Section 3.2).
- r8 (lags, predetermined regressors, dynamic panels): no note in this set records a lagged-dependent-variable specification, a Nickell-bias discussion or a panel-index declaration, and no card records a dynamic-panel estimator; one note records a footnoted check that adds fixed effects varying by employment status to a hazard regression (qje_140_1_11 p.34-34 Section V, footnote 27). This rule therefore rests on the method literature alone and has no corpus support in either layer. It is included because the code layer cannot rule the problem out either: the cards record no lag construction facet at all, so absence here is a recording gap rather than evidence that dynamics are absent.
- r9 (staggered binary treatment is a different paradigm): the boundary between `panel_fe` and `did` is a reader and card-writer judgement, and the concordance in `reported.md` shows it moving: the estimator item is `agree` 24 times but `disagree` once, where a paper names a test difference-in-differences and the card classifies it under a return-event design (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223), and `paper_only` 3 times, including a litigation analysis the paper describes as difference-in-differences with no matching card specification (aer_115_1_8). 4/19 notes record `design_variant_named`. The rule's practical content is the routing decision, which no note records directly.
- r10 (table content and notes): 8/19 notes record `fe_listed_in_table`, 7/19 `se_type_in_notes`, 7/19 `cluster_level_in_notes`, 5/19 `n_reported`, 5/19 `baseline_then_variants`, 2/19 `r2_reported` against 2/19 `other:r2_not_reported`, 1/19 `mean_of_dependent_reported` plus 1/19 `other:dep_mean_in_summary_table`, and 2/19 `weights_stated`. Pointers: rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.32-33 Section 5.5; Equation (5); Table 11 (controls and entity and time fixed effects as Yes rows, observations reported); rfs_2025_rfs_hhaf065 p.32-32 Table 6 (adjusted R-squared and observations per column); restud_2025_restud_rdae072 p.9-9 Table 4 and restud_2025_restud_rdaf104 p.11-25 Table 1; Tables 2 to 4 (neither R-squared nor dependent-variable mean). The clearest divergence is the outcome mean: 1/19 notes record it in the regression table here, against `_all:` `mean_of_dependent_reported` 56/91 across all methods, so this is a `panel_fe` gap rather than a general one. R-squared reporting is split within this set and across the corpus (`_all:` `r2_reported` 36/91), which is why r10 asks for a label rather than for the statistic. No note records the number of groups per absorbed dimension, and the weighting row is recorded twice, once as an explicit statement that no weights are used (aer_113_1_1 p.10-24 Table notes throughout).

## Open questions for review

- Scope boundary with `did`. The paradigm is defined negatively (panel FE without a named quasi-experiment), and r9 exists only to police that boundary. A reviewer must decide whether a staggered binary regressor should be excluded from this paradigm outright, or kept with a mandatory decomposition. The card and note evidence does not settle it: the estimator concordance disagrees once and is `paper_only` 3 times on exactly this kind of specification.
- Concordance totals (`reported.md`, "Paper versus code", 19 notes, 239 items): agree 121, paper_only 83, unknown 20, disagree 9, code_only 6. `paper_only` is concentrated in diagnostic (11), robustness (10), data_source (9), outputs (9), sample (9), comparison_group (7) and fixed_effects (7).
