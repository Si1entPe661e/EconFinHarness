# Recommended rules: `return_event_study` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the event-study method literature listed under "Method sources" and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (3 validated recipe cards: jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809, jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223, rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056; 2 partial, 1 minimal; 2 specifications in total) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (5 paper-practice notes, all 5 with a linked recipe card, but only 2 of those cards, jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 and rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056, were distilled into this method's observed layer; the card behind jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 has no note in this set). The evidence base is thin: shares in both layers are not meaningful, the cards carry no design variants and no specification-to-output links, and the two layers overlap on two papers only. Every corpus pointer below is a pointer to a place, not a count that supports a rule. The rules therefore lean on the method literature and are deliberately few. Observed frequency is evidence of practice, never of correctness; there is no majority threshold; a null card field or a missing note tag means "not recorded".

## Applicability

- Covered: short-window event studies of abnormal stock returns around dated announcements (firm-level or market-wide events), with an expected-return model estimated on a pre-event window (market model, market-adjusted returns, three-, four-, five- or six-factor models), cumulative abnormal returns (CAR) as the outcome, tests of the mean CAR, and cross-sectional regressions of CAR on event or firm characteristics. Long-horizon abnormal returns (buy-and-hold abnormal returns and calendar-time portfolio regressions) are covered by one rule (r5) as a separate branch.
- Not covered / different branch: panel event studies around staggered treatment adoption with unit and time fixed effects and leads and lags (`harness/paradigms/event_study`, with `did`); difference-in-differences around a single date; regression-discontinuity style event windows that condition on a running variable (one note in this set describes such a design at rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.43-43 Section 4.6, tagged `other:rd_like_regression`; treat it under `rdd` for the running-variable part); bond, option or currency event studies (the expected-return models differ); trading-volume or volatility event studies; intraday event studies. Two of the five notes (jfe_2025_j_jfineco_2025_104150, rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045) use the return event study as one of several designs, and the concordance table in `reported.md` includes items from their other designs (bandwidth, difference-in-differences fixed effects), so concordance totals are not specific to this method.

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 5 notes in `reported.md`; a note that lacks a tag did not record it. Pointers are artid, page range and locator as printed in `reported.md`; re-read the page before citing. No note in this set is held out, so no exclusion applies.

- r1 (design statement in one place): 1/5 notes record `estimand_named` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.6-7 Section 4.1), 4/5 record `outcome_definition` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.6-8 Section 4.1 and Table 2 notes; rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.14-15 Section 2.2.1, Table 1 notes) and 1/5 `treatment_definition` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.5-6 Section 3.2 and Section 4.1, equation (1)). Practice diverges from the rule in that the design_statement category is present in only 2/5 notes and the estimation window and the minimum-observation rule are recorded only in a card feature (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 d1), not in any note tag; the readers did not record whether the papers state these items, so this is a recording gap as much as a practice gap.
- r2 (pre-event estimation window, alternative expected-return models): 1/5 notes record `other:market_adjusted_returns` (rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.42-43 Section 4.6, footnote 20) and 1/5 `alt_estimator` together with `alt_specification` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.8-12 Discussion of Appendix Tables A.3, A.9, A.13-A.15, A.17-A.18). The card for jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 (d1) records two expected-return models as design features; the note for the same paper places the alternative return transformations in an appendix summarised in the text, which matches the rule's "cited appendix table" branch. The concordance table lists the robustness item as paper_only in 5 cases, so the observed layer cannot be read as evidence that alternative models were not run.
- r3 (test statistic from the source of uncertainty; cluster level stated and justified): the inference_justification category is present in 2/5 notes; 2/5 record `no_justification_found` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.31-33 Tables 10 and 11; rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf081 p.14-36 Notes to Tables 3, 4, 6, 7, 8, 9), 1/5 `cluster_at_unit_level`, 1/5 `twoway_cluster`, 1/5 `cluster_level_in_notes` and 1/5 `se_type_in_notes` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.8-15 Notes to Tables 2, 3, 6, 7, 8), 2/5 `notes_state_se`. No note records a standardised cross-sectional test, a cross-sectional-correlation adjustment or a rank test, and no note records the number of distinct event dates; the reader gaps list two papers with no clustering justification. Practice diverges from the rule on both counts: the cluster level, where stated, is the firm or the security (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 d4 clusters by permno in code while the note records no statement in the text, concordance item cluster_level code_only), and the mean-CAR test statistic is not named in any note.
- r4 (overlap and confounding-event rule; calendar-time companion): 3/5 notes record `exclusions_justified` (jfe_2025_j_jfineco_2025_104150 p.19-19 Section 5.4; Table 11 notes; rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.42-43 Footnote 21, Table 10 notes), 1/5 `alt_sample` (rfs_2024_rfs_hhae045 p.43-44 Table 10 columns 5-6, Table IA-10) and 2/5 `placebo_outcomes` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.8-9 Discussion around Table 2 and Appendix Table A.7; rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.14-14 footnote 10). One note records a confounding-filing exclusion of the kind the rule asks for; no note records an overlap check or a calendar-time portfolio companion for a short-window study, and no card records either step. The rule goes beyond recorded practice here.
- r5 (long-horizon branch: BHAR and calendar-time regression, HAC lag stated): no note in this method's set carries a long-horizon tag; the only corpus evidence is the card jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 (s18: Newey-West HAC in a monthly calendar-time factor regression; x1: pre-trend plot not found in scope) and the cross-method note of the same paper in `_all/reported.md` (_all:jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-13 Tables 4-5, 9-11, Figs. 3-6, `newey_west`, 4/91 notes across all methods). The reader gaps in `reported.md` include one entry that the Newey-West lag length is never stated in the text, which is the divergence the rule addresses. For the weighting variants, 1/5 notes record the weighting sensitivity in the main text (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.15-33 Section 2.1; Section 3.5) and the concordance item weights is agree for that paper.
- r6 (table contents, magnitude reference, joint-hypothesis limitation): 1/5 notes record `sample_size_reported_per_table` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809 p.8-14 Tables 2-8), 1/5 `n_reported` and `fe_listed_in_table` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.32-33 Tables 10 and 11), 1/5 `mean_of_dependent_reported` (rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf081 p.34-36 Table 8 panel A; Table 9 panel A), 3/5 `compared_to_literature`, 1/5 each of `monetary_terms`, `sd_units`, `relative_to_mean` and `other:calculation_shown_in_footnote` (rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf081 p.33-37 Section 4.1 and 4.2; footnotes 10 to 15), 1/5 `identification_caveat` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.6-34 Introduction; Section 4) and 2/5 `measurement` limitations. Practice diverges in that no note records the number of distinct event dates in a table and no note records the joint-hypothesis caveat in the form the rule asks for; the reader gaps also record a missing mean-of-dependent row in one paper. Practice goes beyond the rule in showing the magnitude calculation in footnotes and in benchmarking against a second coefficient in the same regression (rfs_2026_rfs_hhaf081 p.33-37 Section 4.1, 4.2, `other:benchmark_against_nonculture_tone`).

## Open questions for review

- Whether r3's default for cross-sectional CAR regressions should be date-level clustering (shared market shocks) or firm-level clustering (repeated events per firm) when both are plausible and one dimension has few groups; the corpus shows firm or security clustering only, and no method source in the list settles the two-way case for event studies specifically. The inference-clustering skill's few-cluster guidance (wild cluster bootstrap, aggregation to the date level) is a candidate, but it has not been tested on event-study data here.
- Whether r4 should require the calendar-time companion for every short-window study or only when the overlap check finds clustered dates; the current text requires it whenever dates cluster, which leaves the threshold for "cluster" to the analyst.
- Whether r5's two long-horizon estimators should both be mandatory or whether one may be the stated baseline with the other in an appendix; the sources differ in emphasis and the corpus has one calendar-time card and no BHAR card.
- r2's market-wide-event exception (the market return absorbs the event) needs a source; it is stated from the mechanics of the market model, not from a cited paper.
- The expected-return model list in r2 stops at six factors; whether characteristic-adjusted benchmarks (size and book-to-market portfolios) belong in r2 or only in r5 is undecided.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 5 notes, 60 items): agree 28, paper_only 31, code_only 1, disagree 0, unknown 0. paper_only is concentrated in robustness (5), data_source (4), diagnostic (4), fixed_effects (4), sample (4) and outputs (3). Implication: a null card field is a recording gap, not absence; the observed layer for this method cannot be cited as evidence that a diagnostic, robustness check or sample rule was skipped. The estimator and se_type items (agree 7 and 5, disagree 0) are the fields a downstream user may trust from the cards; the single code_only item (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 cluster by permno in code, no statement in the text) is the case r3 is written for. Several concordance items belong to the other designs in the same papers (bandwidth, difference-in-differences fixed effects, comparison group), so the totals overstate what is known about the return event-study specifications.
- Two of the three cards (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223, rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056) are partial and one (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2024_103809) is minimal; the two specification records are a 2SLS calendar-time regression and an OLS CAR regression, so no card records the mean-CAR test itself. The reviewer should decide whether this method needs at least one full-coverage card before any rule can be marked validated.
