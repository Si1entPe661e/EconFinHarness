# Recommended rules: `ml_nuisance` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (2 validated recipe cards, 2 designs, 8 specifications; one card is `minimal` coverage and one `partial`) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (2 paper-practice notes, both with a linked card). The evidence base is very thin: both papers are AER, both packages are R, one paper is a methods note whose empirical illustration has no results table in the main text, and the other uses double machine learning only as a variant beside fixed-effects regressions. Shares in the two generated files are therefore not meaningful and are used here only as pointers. Observed and reported frequencies are evidence of practice, never of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a null card field or an absent note tag means "not recorded", not "not done". The five rules below rest mainly on the method literature; the corpus shows where practice already matches a rule and where it is silent. Cross-method pointers are prefixed `_all:` and come from `harness/paradigms/_all/reported.md` (91 notes). The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; historical build and review records are not distributed.

## Applicability

- Covered: estimation of a low-dimensional causal or structural parameter (ATE, ATT or ATTE, partially linear regression coefficient, partially linear IV coefficient) when nuisance functions (outcome regressions, propensity score, first-stage or instrument projections) are estimated with machine learning; double or debiased machine learning with Neyman-orthogonal scores and cross-fitting; post-double-selection lasso for a treatment coefficient with high-dimensional controls; causal forests and related honest ML estimators of conditional or group average treatment effects; cross-section, pooled or clustered data.
- Not covered or a different branch: prediction-only ML with no causal parameter (`predictive_regression`); ML used to construct a proxy or index that then enters an ordinary regression; text-as-data pipelines; reinforcement or bandit designs; Bayesian nonparametrics; synthetic control and matrix completion (`did` and `synthetic_control` branches); the panel fixed-effects specifications that a paper reports beside its DML columns (`panel_fe`).

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

- r1 (estimand, score, identification and overlap stated first): 2/2 notes record `estimand_named` (aer_107_5_51 p.1-1 Section I, para 1) and `identifying_assumption_explicit` (aer_107_5_51 p.1-1 Section I, para 1, eq. (1)-(2); aer_113_3_2 p.10-11 Section III); 1/2 record `other:nuisance_function_definition` (aer_107_5_51 p.2-2 Section I, discussion before eq. (5)-(6)) and `other:overlap_restriction` (aer_107_5_51 p.3-3 Assumption 1(ii)). Practice diverges from the rule in aer_113_3_2: the paper names the DML framework it follows (aer_113_3_2 p.14-14 Section III, footnote 22) but its identification statement is written for the fixed-effects comparison, and no note records a separate estimand statement for the DML columns; whether the DML and FE columns target the same estimand is not recorded.
- r2 (cross-fitting, learner, tuning and software stated): 2/2 notes record `baseline_then_variants` and `alt_estimator`, in aer_107_5_51 as a generic claim that the method is valid for any qualifying learner (aer_107_5_51 p.2-2 Section II, para 1) and the algorithm is given as steps (aer_107_5_51 p.2-3 Section II.A, Steps 1-3); 2/2 record `software_named`, but for aer_107_5_51 the reader's statement is that no software is named anywhere in the printed note (aer_107_5_51 p.1-4 throughout), so the tag is carried by a negative observation. The rate conditions on the learners are stated only as high-level assumptions (aer_107_5_51 p.3-3 Assumption 1(iii) and following discussion). Neither note records K, the seed, or an out-of-fold fit measure; that is the part of the rule where practice, as recorded, falls short.
- r3 (repeated splits, split-adjusted variance): 1/2 notes record `other:sample_splitting_variance` (aer_107_5_51 p.4-4 Section III, para 1), `other:repeated_splitting` (aer_107_5_51 p.4-4 Section III, para 2), `other:mean_vs_median_aggregation` (aer_107_5_51 p.4-4 Section III) and `other:variance_inflation_for_splitting` (aer_107_5_51 p.4-4 Section III, closing paragraphs), with the whole section framed as robustness to the split (aer_107_5_51 p.4-4 Section III heading and content). Outside this method, one note records medians over many splits with a split-uncertainty adjustment (_all: restud_2026_restud_rdag029 p.20-23 Footnote 9 and footnote 11). The aer_113_3_2 note records nothing about repeated partitions for its DML columns, so the rule is supported by the methods note and untested against applied practice in this corpus.
- r4 (score variance, cluster level, fold construction by cluster): 2/2 notes record `no_justification_found` (aer_107_5_51 p.3-3 Section II; aer_113_3_2 p.13-16 Table 3 notes; Table 4 notes); 1/2 record `other:analytic_asymptotic_se` (aer_107_5_51 p.3-3 Section II, para after eq. (7)), `cluster_at_assignment_level` and `se_type_in_notes` (aer_113_3_2 p.16-16 Table 4 notes; Table 5 notes). Practice diverges from the rule in both directions: aer_113_3_2 states the cluster level for its FE tables but, as the reader's gap says, "No stated standard-error or clustering description is given anywhere for the double/debiased machine-learning specifications", and the card records an `iid` variance there; aer_107_5_51 gives the analytic score variance but no clustering discussion. No note in this method mentions cluster-level fold assignment; the rule's fold-construction clause rests on the method sources alone. A related reader gap (aer_113_3_2) notes that no multiple-testing adjustment is described across many outcome-by-specification combinations.
- r5 (honest ML heterogeneity, reported with intervals and labelled exploratory): 1/2 notes record `heterogeneity_reported`, and only as split-sample and appendix exercises for the FE design, labelled exploratory (aer_113_3_2 p.32-32 Section VIB, footnote referencing online Appendix Table); the methods note states that its model allows full heterogeneity but reports none (reader gap). Cross-method, 3/91 notes record `ml_heterogeneity` (_all: aer_107_5_108 p.1-1 Introduction; _all: ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.33-34 Table IV; Figure 4), 1/91 records `other:split_sensitivity` (_all: aer_107_5_108 p.5-5 Section IV) and 1/91 an internal-consistency check between the ML estimator's pooled effect and a simpler estimator (_all: ecta_2025_ecta19303 p.32-33 Section 6.2). The rule is therefore written from the literature; the corpus supplies pointers, not support.

## Open questions for review

- Evidence base: 2 cards and 2 notes, one of which is a methods note (aer_107_5_51) whose illustration is not a substantive application and whose card is `minimal` coverage. The reviewer should decide whether rules r1 to r4 may cite that paper both as a method source (by DOI) and as corpus practice (by artid and page), or whether one of the two roles should be dropped to avoid counting the same text twice.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 2 notes, 23 items): agree 19, unknown 2, paper_only 1, disagree 1, code_only 0. The single `disagree` is the design variant of aer_107_5_51 (card `dml_plr`, paper text an interactive model with an AIPW-style score). Implication: the `design.variant` facet of `observed.md` should not be cited as evidence about the score used; r1 relies on the paper text for that. The se_type and cluster_level rows agree in both cards, so those facets can be cited, and the `iid` entry for aer_113_3_2 s3 is a code observation that the paper's silence neither confirms nor contradicts.
- Whether r3's median aggregation should be the default or whether the mean with the matching variance formula should be, and what S the harness should suggest when the plan gives no value; the two sources describe both without ranking them for applied work, and the harness must not invent a number.
- r4's clause that folds be formed at the cluster level comes from the multiway-cluster DML source; whether it should be required or only recommended for a single clustering dimension needs a reviewer decision, and there is no corpus evidence either way.
- r5 cites `_all` pointers only, and two of the cited papers (aer_107_5_108, ecta_2025_ecta19303) are in the iv and rct hold-outs. `ml_nuisance` has no hold-out list, so the pointers are allowed under the `_all:` convention, but the reviewer should confirm this does not contaminate those skills' evaluations.
- Whether post-double-selection lasso belongs in this paradigm or deserves its own branch: the corpus has one PDS-related statement outside this method (_all: jpe_2026_738249 p.22-22 IV. Methods, footnote 23, on the validity of conventional standard errors after lasso selection) and no card; r1 and r2 fold PDS in through their exception columns, which a reviewer may find too compressed.
- Which items of r2 (K, seed, learner, tuning rule, package version, out-of-fold fit) a code-engineer template should emit into `estimates.json` so that the rule becomes testable; no template exists for this method.
- Practice for overlap trimming (r1) is recorded only as a formal assumption in the methods note (aer_107_5_51 p.3-3 Assumption 1(ii)); whether to recommend a specific trimming rule or only require that the rule be stated needs a decision, and the harness should not supply an interval.
