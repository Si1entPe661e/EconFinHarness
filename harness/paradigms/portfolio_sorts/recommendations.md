# Recommended rules: `portfolio_sorts` (draft)

Written by the empirical-analyst role from the method literature listed under "Method sources" and from two generated layers of corpus practice: observed code practice in `observed.md` (4 validated recipe cards, 4 designs, 8 specifications; 3 cards at partial coverage and 1 at minimal coverage, no design variants recorded) and reported paper practice in `reported.md` (4 paper-practice notes taken from the paper text; all 4 notes have a linked card). The evidence base is thin: with fewer than 5 papers, shares in either layer are not meaningful and are used here only as pointers, and no rule below is conditioned on a corpus frequency. Observed and reported frequencies are evidence of practice only, never of correctness; there is no majority threshold, and a tag absent from a note or a null card field means "not recorded", not "not done". Every rule states when it applies, its exceptions, its sources with verification status, corpus pointers (artid + record id from `observed.md`; artid + page + locator from `reported.md`; `_all:` for the cross-method file) and the validation result. The document stays `draft` until its rules have been through an independent check and the open questions below are settled; check records live in `harness_build/pilot/runs/`.

## Applicability

- Covered: univariate and double (independent or conditional) sorts of assets on lagged characteristics with subsequent returns as the outcome; long-short spread portfolios; time-series factor-model regressions of portfolio returns; the choice of breakpoint universe, weighting, rebalancing frequency and holding period; HAC inference on portfolio time series; multiple-testing and implementability caveats. Sorts on an externally supplied membership list (one corpus paper) are treated as a sort with a single formation date per period.
- Not covered / different branch: Fama-MacBeth cross-sectional regressions (the `fama_macbeth` paradigm, even though two corpus cards contain them next to the sorts); GMM tests of factor models and maximum-Sharpe-ratio comparisons (one corpus card records them; they belong to the `gmm` paradigm); event-study designs around announcements (the `event_study` paradigm); machine-learning return prediction where the sort is only a presentation device for a fitted signal; portfolio sorts of bond, currency or fund returns, where the breakpoint and microcap rules below do not transfer without restatement.

## Reported practice behind the rules (paper text)

Counts are papers out of the 4 notes in `reported.md`; a note that lacks a tag did not record it. Pointers are artid, page range and locator as printed in `reported.md`; re-read the page before citing. With 4 notes no share is meaningful; the counts are pointers, not evidence for or against a rule.

- r1 (formation rule stated as the estimand): 4/4 notes record `weights_stated` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.11-13 Tables 6-8; jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.8-9 Section 4.2), 4/4 `sample_period_stated`, 3/4 `exclusions_justified` and 3/4 `unit_of_observation_stated` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.6-7 Section 3), 1/4 `other:nyse_breakpoints_stated` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.10-11 Table 6 note), 2/4 `identifying_assumption_explicit` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.8-14 Section 1.1 end; Section 2 opening and footnote 10, on formation timing). Practice diverges from the rule in two places: one reader gap records that the number of assets per portfolio per period is not reported, and one note records that a paper has no dedicated data section, so the formation rule is scattered across table notes (`other:no_dedicated_data_section`, 1/4). The rule's requirement to state whether double sorts are independent or conditional is recorded only for conditional sorts used as heterogeneity (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.35-43 Tables 13, 15, 16, `mechanism_motivated` 2/4). In the cards, `weights` is paper_only in 2 and unknown in 1 of the concordance items, so the card layer cannot be used to confirm weighting.
- r2 (microcap and weighting variants, size check first): 3/4 notes record `alt_sample` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.16-17 Section 4.5, Appendix Tables A.2-A.3, value-weighting as a robustness variant; jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.10-10 Section 4, footnote 20, a single-sort variant reported as unreported), 2/4 `baseline_then_variants` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.17-18 Table 5), 1/4 `other:size_distribution_check` and 1/4 `summary_stats_table` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.10-12 Table 3; Figure 3; Section 1.2). Practice diverges in direction: two papers use equal weighting as the baseline and value weighting as the variant, which the rule permits only with a stated reason; one paper states the reason (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.8-9 Section 4.2). No note records a microcap-exclusion variant framed as such except through the small-cap exclusion row in rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.17-18 Table 5.
- r3 (alphas from a fixed factor-model set, joint test when spanning is claimed): 2/4 notes record `baseline_then_variants` in specification reporting (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.14-15 Section 3.1; Table 3), 2/4 `intermediate_outcomes` and 1/4 `ruled_out_alternatives` under mechanism evidence (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.35-39 Section 6.1; Tables 13, 14). One note records `other:measure_validation` for the sorting variable before the alphas (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.22-30 Section 5.1 to 5.3; Figures 2, 3, 4; Tables 7, 8, 9) and one records a portfolio-characteristics table before the risk adjustment (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.16-18 Section 3.2; Table 4), both beyond the rule. Practice diverges where the sorts feed a GMM or maximum-Sharpe-ratio comparison instead of alphas (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746, estimator agree in the concordance but out of this paradigm's scope) and where portfolio-level predictive regressions carry factor controls in place of separate alphas (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-13 Tables 4-5, 9-11, Figs. 3-6). No note records a GRS or other joint test.
- r4 (HAC with a pre-stated lag rule; panel clustering stated): 2/4 notes record `newey_west` (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-13 Tables 4-5, 9-11, Figs. 3-6; rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.16-20 footnote 9; Section 3.4), 1/4 `in_footnote`, 2/4 `notes_state_se` (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.13-43 Tables 1 to 16 notes), and across methods `_all:` `newey_west` 4/91 (including `_all:jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.7-9 Tables 3-5 notes`). The cards agree with the papers on SE type where both record it (se_type agree 5, code_only 1, paper_only 1). Practice diverges from the rule on the lag: three reader gaps state that the lag is not stated, not justified, or that the clustering dimensions of the panel regressions are not reasoned; one paper states an automatic bandwidth and one a fixed lag, and neither note records a lag sensitivity check.
- r5 (test family declared, adjustment reported, full portfolio pattern shown): 0/4 notes record a multiple-testing adjustment; 1/4 records `no_justification_found` for exactly this reason (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.1-23 throughout), and three reader gaps repeat it for the other papers. Across methods `_all:` `multiple_testing_adjustment` is recorded in 15/91 notes, none of them a portfolio-sort paper. The cards record a multiple-testing diagnostic as not_found_in_scope in one paper (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 x1 in the concordance, agree). Practice diverges from the rule in the other direction too: one note records a one-sided test for a state-split difference (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.31-32 Table 10 notes; Section 5.4) and many sample splits (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.29-32 Section 5.4; Table 10) without a family-wise statement. Full portfolio tables (all deciles plus the spread) are recorded in 1/4 notes (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.14-15 Section 3.1; Table 3).
- r6 (gross-of-cost statement, turnover, identification caveat): 2/4 notes record `identification_caveat` (jfe_2024_j_jfineco_2023_103746 p.2-16 Introduction and Conclusion; rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.6-34 Introduction; Section 4), 2/4 `interpretation_caveat`, 2/4 `measurement`, 1/4 `other:turnover_reported` (rfs_2025_rfs_hhae056 p.9-11 Tables 1 and 2; Figures 1 and 2, which is list turnover rather than portfolio turnover), and 1/4 `restricted_data_used` for a short-lending-fee test (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.39-41 Section 6.2; footnote 17; Table 15 notes). Practice diverges from the rule: no note records a gross-of-cost statement, portfolio turnover, or net-of-cost returns, and one reader gap records the absence of any back-of-envelope economic translation of the spread. Magnitudes are interpreted relative to a reference period (rfs_2026_rfs_hhag042 p.32-38 Section 5.4; Section 6.1) or in standard-deviation units of the sorting shock (jfe_2026_j_jfineco_2025_104223 p.9-16 throughout Section 4), which the rule does not address.

## Open questions for review

- Evidence base: 4 cards and 4 notes from 2 journals (JFE, RFS), all with cards at partial or minimal coverage and no design variants recorded. No rule can be conditioned on corpus frequency; every corpus pointer is illustrative. A reviewer should decide whether to keep the rule set at six until more cards are accepted.
- Paradigm boundaries: two of the four cards mix portfolio sorts with Fama-MacBeth regressions, GMM factor tests and an event-study-style inclusion test (concordance: estimator disagree 1, where the paper names a difference-in-differences and the card records a return event study). The reviewer should confirm that r1 to r6 exclude those branches and that the `fama_macbeth` and `gmm` paradigms cover them.
- Concordance between paper statements and recipe cards (`reported.md`, 4 notes, 50 items): agree 27, paper_only 18, code_only 3, disagree 1, unknown 1. paper_only is concentrated in robustness (4), sample (4), weights (2), other (2) and single items in comparison_group, data_source, diagnostic, fixed_effects, outputs and se_type. Implication: card fields for sample restrictions, weights, robustness and diagnostics are recording gaps and must not be read as absence; observed.md cannot be used to say that a paper did not report a value-weighted variant or a microcap exclusion. Estimator (agree 7, code_only 1, disagree 1) and se_type (agree 5) are the card fields that can be trusted most, and the code_only items (a cluster option and an iid first-step SE in the code that the paper does not state) are exactly the traces a reviewer should follow under the inference-clustering skill.
- r4 lag rule: the rule offers a fixed a priori lag or an automatic bandwidth. The reviewer should decide whether the harness recommends one default, and whether "holding period minus one" is the right minimum for overlapping cohorts; no corpus note justifies its lag.
- r5 adjustment: which adjustment (t-threshold, false-discovery-rate control, bootstrap over the family) the harness recommends as the default is not decided; the verified source argues for a higher threshold, the others are unverified.
- r2 microcap definition: the NYSE size percentile used to define microcaps is a convention taken from the literature and is not fixed here; the reviewer should decide whether the rule names one.
- Sources: the 14 DOIs whose Crossref responses were lost in the first pass were re-queried on 2026-09-08 and all resolved with matching title and first author, so they now carry `verified_online`; no source in this file is left unverified. The three verified DOIs resolve to online-first years on Crossref (2018 for Hou, Xue and Zhang; 2015 for Harvey, Liu and Zhu); the print years are used in the tables.
- Significance stars: 3/4 notes record `stars_used`; no rule is written, the writer role's convention decides.
- Placement tags (`in_main_text`, `in_appendix`, `in_footnote`) are reader judgements about the paper text and cannot be validated against code; the placement clauses in r2 (size check before the return tests) and r6 (limitations paragraph) rest on them alone.
